Organ field guide · the liver

The refinery under your ribs

Everything you swallow passes through it before it reaches the rest of you.

It sorts, stores, builds, burns, cleans and recycles. Over 500 separate jobs, in one organ the size of a rugby ball.

Job 1 · Gatekeeper

Gut blood doesn't go to your heart. It goes here first.

The liver sits on the only road out of your gut, and inspects every delivery.

Under the hood

  • Dual supply: portal vein ≈75% of flow but only ~50% of oxygen; hepatic artery ≈25% of flow, the other ~50% of O₂.
  • ~25% of cardiac output (~1.5 L/min) for an organ that is ~2% of body weight.
  • First-pass effect: swallow morphine and only ~⅓ survives the liver. That's why IV doses are smaller and nitroglycerin goes under the tongue (skips the portal vein).
  • Arterial buffer response: if portal flow drops, adenosine builds up and the hepatic artery dilates to compensate.
  • Portal pressure gradient normally 1–5 mmHg. ≥10 mmHg = portal hypertension → blood detours into fragile varices in the oesophagus.
Job 2 · The assembly line

Hundreds of thousands of tiny hexagons

Blood trickles in from the corners and drains out the middle. Cells along the way do different jobs.

Periportal: first in line

Oxygen: highest · ~60–65 mmHg

Gets blood first, so it gets the most oxygen and the freshest nutrients. Runs the energy-expensive jobs.

Making glucoseBurning fat (β-oxidation)Urea cycleBile acid uptakeCholesterol synthesis

Under the hood

  • Lobule: ~1 mm hexagon; a human liver has on the order of 10⁵–10⁶ of them.
  • Portal triad = branch of portal vein + hepatic artery + bile duct (plus lymphatics & nerves).
  • Zonation is set by a Wnt/β-catenin signal from the central vein and the oxygen gradient: same cell, different job by address.
  • Countercurrent: blood flows inward, bile flows outward in sealed channels between the cells.
Job 3 · The crew

Five cell types, one sieve

The blood channels here are deliberately leaky, so plasma touches the workers directly.

Under the hood

  • Hepatocytes: ~60% of cells, ~80% of volume. Each packs ~1,000–2,000 mitochondria. Many carry doubled genomes (polyploid) or two nuclei.
  • LSECs (sinusoidal endothelium): sieve plates with 50–150 nm holes and no basement membrane. They also make most of your clotting factor VIII.
  • Kupffer cells: the body's largest macrophage population (~80% of fixed tissue macrophages). They eat gut bacteria, endotoxin and worn-out red cells.
  • Stellate (Ito) cells: sit in the space of Disse holding vitamin A. On injury they turn into myofibroblasts and lay down collagen: the origin of scarring.
  • Bile canaliculi: ~1 µm channels between hepatocytes, sealed by tight junctions, draining toward the corners.
Job 4 · Sugar bank

Your blood sugar never hits zero. That's the liver.

It saves sugar after meals and pays it back between them, minute by minute, for life.

Under the hood

  • Glycogen: ~80–100 g stored in the liver. Muscles hold ~4× more but can't share it: they lack glucose-6-phosphatase, the enzyme that lets glucose out.
  • Target: blood glucose held around 4–6 mmol/L (70–110 mg/dL), fed or fasted.
  • Gluconeogenesis builds glucose from lactate (Cori cycle), alanine from muscle (glucose–alanine cycle) and glycerol from fat.
  • Switches: glucagon → cAMP → PKA → release; insulin → store. The liver also clears roughly half of the insulin the pancreas releases on first pass.
  • Fructose is taken up almost entirely by the liver and skips glycolysis's main brake (PFK-1), so excess flows straight into fat production.
Job 5 · Fat logistics

Burn it, pack it, or park it

Every fat molecule that reaches the liver gets one of three fates. When the "park it" option wins too often, you get fatty liver.

Under the hood

  • Ketogenesis: β-oxidation → acetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA synthase 2 → acetoacetate & β-hydroxybutyrate. Altruistic: the liver lacks the enzyme SCOT, so it can't eat its own ketones.
  • VLDL export needs apoB-100 and the MTP protein. Block it and fat gets trapped inside.
  • Cholesterol: the liver is a main producer (rate step: HMG-CoA reductase). Statins block it → cells sense low cholesterol (SREBP2) → more LDL receptors → LDL pulled out of blood.
  • PCSK9 destroys LDL receptors; drugs that block PCSK9 cut LDL by ~50–60%.
  • Alcohol's trick: metabolising it floods the cell with NADH, which halts fat burning and favours fat building.
Job 6 · Poison disposal

Turning brain poison into pee

Every protein you digest leaves ammonia behind. The liver spins it into urea, which is harmless and water-soluble.

Under the hood

  • Five enzymes, two compartments: CPS1 and OTC in mitochondria; ASS1, ASL, ARG1 in cytosol. NAGS is the on-switch for CPS1.
  • Cost: 4 high-energy phosphate bonds per urea molecule. Detox isn't free.
  • Safety net: zone 3 cells carry glutamine synthetase, mopping up any ammonia that slipped past zone 1.
  • Normal blood ammonia < ~35–50 µmol/L. When the liver fails it rises, brain astrocytes swell → hepatic encephalopathy: confusion, sleep reversal, a "flapping" tremor (asterixis).
  • Lactulose treats it by acidifying the colon, trapping ammonia as NH₄⁺ so it leaves in stool.
Job 7 · Chemical plant

Making oily things washable

Most drugs and toxins dissolve in fat, so you can't pee them out. The liver bolts on handles and tags until they dissolve in water.

Under the hood

  • CYP450: 57 human genes. CYP3A4 alone handles roughly a third to a half of all drugs.
  • Genes matter: CYP2D6 ranges from "poor" to "ultra-rapid"; ultra-rapid people turn codeine into dangerous amounts of morphine. NAT2 makes you a fast or slow "acetylator".
  • Phase II tags: glucuronic acid (UGT), sulfate (SULT), glutathione (GST), acetyl (NAT), methyl groups.
  • Phase III pumps: MRP2 and P-glycoprotein push conjugates into bile.
  • Induction: smoking revs up CYP1A2, rifampicin and St John's wort rev up CYP3A4. Grapefruit inhibits CYP3A4 in the gut wall and raises some drug levels.
  • Paracetamol: its toxic by-product NAPQI is cleaned by glutathione. Run out, and zone 3 cells die. Antidote N-acetylcysteine refills glutathione; best within ~8 h.
Job 7b · Alcohol

One drink an hour, no shortcuts

Under the hood

  • Zero-order kinetics: above low levels the rate stays fixed, regardless of how much you drank.
  • ALDH2*2 variant (common in East Asia) stalls step 2 → acetaldehyde builds → red face, racing heart.
  • Heavy drinking induces CYP2E1 (MEOS), which makes more free radicals and more NAPQI from paracetamol.
Job 8 · Protein factory

Most proteins in your blood were built here

The stuff that makes blood clot, holds water inside your vessels, and ferries drugs around.

Under the hood

  • Albumin: ~10–15 g made daily, ~55–60% of plasma protein. Creates most of the oncotic pull holding water in vessels; low albumin → swollen legs, fluid in the belly (ascites). Also carries bilirubin, fatty acids, calcium, drugs.
  • Clotting: fibrinogen and factors II, V, VII, IX, X, XI, XIII, plus anticoagulants protein C, S and antithrombin. II, VII, IX, X need vitamin K (γ-carboxylation): what warfarin blocks.
  • Acute phase: in infection, CRP can rise ~1000×; hepcidin locks iron away from bacteria.
  • Also: angiotensinogen (blood pressure), IGF-1, thrombopoietin (platelets), transferrin, ceruloplasmin, α1-antitrypsin (protects lungs), complement proteins.
Job 9 · Bile

Soap for fat, and a rubbish chute

Bile breaks fat into droplets you can absorb, and carries out waste that's too big for the kidneys.

Under the hood

  • 0.5–1 L of bile a day. Bile acids are made from cholesterol by CYP7A1, then conjugated to glycine or taurine.
  • Enterohepatic circulation: the ileum's ASBT transporter reclaims ~95%; only ~0.5 g/day is replaced.
  • Bile acids are hormones too: via FXR they make the gut release FGF19 (telling the liver to stop making more); via TGR5 they trigger GLP-1 and energy use.
  • Waste out: bilirubin, excess cholesterol, copper, some drugs. Copper can't leave → Wilson disease.

Job 12 · Regrowth

Cut off two-thirds. It grows back.

The only human internal organ that can rebuild its own mass. The Greeks had a myth about it: Prometheus's liver regrew every night.

Under the hood

  • Not a new lobe: the remaining lobes enlarge (compensatory hyperplasia). Shape is lost, mass and function return.
  • Priming: TNF-α and IL-6 wake hepatocytes from G0. Growth: HGF (via c-Met) and EGF drive 1–2 rounds of division. Mostly existing hepatocytes do it, not stem cells.
  • Hepatostat: growth stops when liver mass returns to ~2–2.5% of body weight, via the Hippo/YAP pathway and TGF-β.
  • Living donation works because of this: donor and recipient each end up with a near full-sized liver. A child's graft grows with the child; an oversized graft shrinks.
Job 13 · Silence

It doesn't complain until it's late

No pain nerves inside and huge spare capacity. Damage can build for years with no symptoms.

Healthy

baseline

Smooth, soft, red-brown. Fat under 5% of cells.

Under the hood

  • MASLD (formerly NAFLD) affects roughly 1 in 3 adults worldwide.
  • Weight loss works: ~7–10% body weight loss can resolve MASH; ≥10% can shrink fibrosis.
  • Checks: FIB-4 score (age, AST, ALT, platelets) and FibroScan (liver stiffness, kPa) find scarring long before symptoms.

The one-line version

A refinery, a bank, a factory and a bin, all in one wet organ.

It sits between your gut and the rest of you. It decides what gets in, keeps your blood sugar steady, makes your clotting and carrier proteins, turns poisons into things you can pee or poop out, stores years of vitamins, and rebuilds itself when damaged. It does all of this silently, which is why looking after it matters before it complains.
