People rated 70 statements about themselves. Then they rated how satisfied they are with life. This chart shows which statements travel with which answer.

How to read it

The big picture

Three things the chart says

Mood beats virtue

The top is fun, energy, calm. “Loyal”, “work hard”, “improve myself” sit near the bottom.

The downside is louder

The strongest unhappy items (−0.7) outweigh the strongest happy ones (+0.6). Feeling bad says more than feeling good.

Being seen matters

Top of the unhappy side: misunderstood, unexcited, afraid of being alone. Connection, not achievement.

Before you quote it

It’s correlation. Fun may cause satisfaction, satisfaction may cause fun, or both come from a steady temperament.

Some items are half the answer. “Nothing excites me” is close to saying “I’m not satisfied”. Strong numbers there are partly overlap.

Source: Mõttus et al., Most people’s life satisfaction matches their personality traits, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology (2024). Self- and informant ratings from Estonian and international samples. Values above are read off the thick line of the published figure and rounded; the dashed lines and large type in the original mark further analyses defined in the paper.
