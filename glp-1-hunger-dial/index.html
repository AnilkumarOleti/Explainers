GLP-1 · Model W-1 · Hunger receiver

GLP-1 turns down the volume on hunger.

Your gut already makes this hormone. The drugs are a long-lasting copy of it. Turn the dial.

Drug names you'll hear: semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus), tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound), liraglutide (Saxenda, Victoza).

Food noise monitor

No drug: thoughts about food keep popping up.

1 · The signal

After you eat, your gut texts your body: "food's here."

Your own GLP-1 · one burst

Gone in minutes

An enzyme (DPP-4) cuts it up. Half is gone in about 2 minutes.

Drug copy · one shot a week

Lasts all week

Built so the enzyme can't cut it. Semaglutide's half-life is about 7 days.

Same message. The drug keeps it switched on, day and night.

2 · Where the message lands

One message, three places.

  1. 1

    Brain

    Hunger gets quieter. Cravings and constant food thoughts fade.

  2. 2

    Stomach

    Food leaves more slowly, so you feel full sooner and longer.

  3. 3

    Pancreas

    Releases insulin when blood sugar is high, and less of the hormone that raises sugar. That's why these started as diabetes drugs.

Tirzepatide also copies a second gut hormone, GIP. That's part of why it tends to cause more weight loss.

Better blood pressure and blood sugar also slide back toward where they were.

Why? The drug holds the dial. It doesn't move where the dial wants to rest.

Your body defends its usual weight. After you lose weight, hunger hormones rise and you burn a bit less energy. The drug masks that pull. Remove it and the pull wins. Doctors now treat obesity like blood pressure: something you manage, not cure.

What seems to help when stopping

Plan the exit with a doctor Keep strength training Eat enough protein Keep the new eating habits Some stay on a lower dose or switch treatment

A 2026 study of ~8,000 real patients found smaller average regain than in trials, mostly because many restarted or moved to another treatment.

Watch-outs

  • Common

    Stomach upset

    Nausea, vomiting, constipation, diarrhoea. Worst early and after each dose increase.

  • Plan for it

    Muscle loss

    Around a third of the weight lost can be muscle and other lean tissue. Protein and strength training protect it.

  • Uncommon

    Gallstones

    More likely with fast weight loss.

  • Rare

    Pancreatitis

    Severe belly pain spreading to the back means go to hospital.

  • Rare

    Eyes

    A rare optic-nerve stroke (NAION) is now on semaglutide's EU label. In diabetes, fast sugar drops can briefly worsen eye disease.

  • Precaution

    Thyroid & pregnancy

    Caused thyroid tumours in rodents, unclear in humans. Avoided with a family history of medullary thyroid cancer, and in pregnancy.

Largely cleared

Suicidal thoughts: the US FDA removed this warning in January 2026 after data on 100,000+ trial patients showed no rise.

Pancreatic cancer: long trials have not shown a higher risk.

Still unknown

GLP-1 drugs have been used for diabetes since 2005. High weight-loss doses in large numbers of people only started around 2021, so most data covers under 5 years. Lifelong use is still being studied.

Years of experience
₹

In India: semaglutide's patent ended on 20 March 2026. Dozens of generics followed, many around ₹1,300–4,200 a month. It is still prescription-only, and needs a doctor to start, dose and stop it safely.
