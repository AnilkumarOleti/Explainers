Why AI says wrong things so confidently

The missing gauge

A chatbot is a fluency machine. Its dashboard has a dial for how smooth an answer sounds, and an empty slot where “I don’t know” should be.

Try it

One steady voice, right or wrong

Pick a question. The fitted dial barely moves. The dashed dial is the one you wish it had.

You ask

What was the population of Ouagadougou in 1904?

It answers

The population was 8,312.

A tidy number, likely invented. Records that old are thin.

Illustrative answers and readings, not a recording of a real chatbot.

The engine

Its one job is to pick the next word

A language model writes one word at a time. For every gap it scores each candidate, then prints one.

The doubt lives in the bars. The printed sentence leaves it behind.

Illustrative numbers.

The voice

It learned to talk from people who were sure

Its training text is books, articles and answers. People write down what they know. Hardly anyone publishes “no idea”, so its default voice is a sure one.

The test

Guessing scores higher

Models are tuned against tests. On most of them “I don’t know” earns zero and a lucky guess earns a point. Train on that and you get a guesser.

Behind the panel

A faint signal exists. The wire is loose.

Researchers looking inside models find something like “I recognise this”. It is weak, and a name that only sounds familiar can trip it.

Newer models are trained to heed it more. They still miss.

What to do

Until it is fitted, you are the gauge

  • Check the brittle bits

    Names, numbers, dates, quotes, links. Bluffs cluster there.

  • Hand it the source

    Paste the document in. Reading beats remembering.

  • Ask where it’s from

    Then open that source yourself. Citations get invented too.

  • Allow “I don’t know”

    Say so in your prompt. It lowers the pressure to guess.
