Half-marathon nutrition

Fuel for 21.1

Your legs carry enough fast fuel for about 90 minutes of hard running. Most half marathons take longer. Nutrition is how you close that gap.

The big idea

You run on two tanks

Carbs are the race fuel.
Quick to burn, but the tank is small.

Fat is the backup.
Huge tank, slow flow. Running on it alone means slowing down.

The job: top up the small tank before and during the race.

What happens when it runs dry

The wall

Carbs run low and your pace falls off a cliff. Runners call it hitting the wall. Eating on the run slows the drain.

What the race costs

About 1 kcal per kg per km

Running costs roughly the same energy per kilometre whether you run fast or slow. Slower runners just spend it over more hours, so they need more fuel on the course.

Water and salt

Drink to thirst, not to a schedule

You will lose some water. That's normal. Drinking so much that you gain weight is the risky direction: it dilutes the salt in your blood.

Start70.0kg · before the gun

Sip, don't chug

A few mouthfuls at each water station is plenty for most people.

Salt in the heat

Hot, humid race or salt-crusted kit? Add a salt tab or sports drink.

Warning signs

Headache, nausea, confusion or puffy hands after lots of water: stop drinking and get medical help.

Train your gut

Nothing new on race day

Your stomach learns to handle fuel on the move, the same way your legs learn the distance. Practise on every long run.
