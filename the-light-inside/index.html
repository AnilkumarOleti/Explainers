An explainer · Consciousness science, 2026

The light inside

Your brain is about 86 billion neurons trading electrical spikes. Somehow, it also feels like something to be you.

This page walks from the puzzle, to the tools scientists use, to nine theories in technical detail: mechanism, signature measurements, landmark experiments, and where each one breaks. Each section opens with a picture, then goes deeper.

01 · The puzzle

Easy problem, hard problem

Easy problems: how the brain discriminates red, routes it, remembers it, says "red". Hard engineering, but standard science.

Hard problem: why all that processing is accompanied by an inner felt quality at all.

Four distinctions do most of the work in this field. Learn them and the theories become much easier to place.

Nagel · 1974"What it is like"A system is conscious if there is something it is like to be it. A bat's sonar world is real to the bat, even if we can't imagine it.
Block · 1995Phenomenal vs accessPhenomenal: the raw felt quality. Access: information available for reasoning, memory and report. Theories split on whether these can come apart.
Clinical usageLevel vs contentLevel: how conscious you are (coma to fully awake). Content: what you are conscious of (a face, a pain, a thought).
Chalmers · 1995 / 2018Hard vs meta-problemHard: why there is experience. Meta: why we say there is a hard problem. Some theories only aim at the second.
02 · The dimmer switch

Awake isn't the same as aware

After Steven Laureys (2005). Normal states fall on the diagonal. Dreaming sits above it: low arousal, rich experience. Clinical states fall below it: eyes open, sleep–wake cycles intact, but little or no sign of awareness.

This plot is why consciousness science matters outside philosophy. A patient in the unresponsive wakefulness syndrome (formerly "vegetative state") opens their eyes but never follows commands. Are they experiencing anything? Bedside behaviour can't tell you. Theories that predict which brain signals mark awareness could.

03 · The toolkit

Switch the light on and off

The contrastive method. Hold the stimulus constant, let awareness vary, and subtract the brain activity. What's left is a candidate neural correlate of consciousness (NCC). Crick and Koch (1990) defined the NCC as the minimal set of neural mechanisms jointly sufficient for a specific conscious percept. The difficulty is that the subtraction also catches things that merely come before awareness (prerequisites) or after it (consequences, like preparing to report).

Paradigm 1 · Backward masking. Try it.
 

A word, then a pattern mask right after. Below roughly 40–50 ms the mask usually wins: your visual cortex still processes the word (it can prime later responses), but it never reaches awareness.

Binocular rivalry. Input is fixed; perception flips every few seconds. A clean way to track awareness without changing the stimulus.

Sperling (1960). People seem to see the whole grid but can only report about four letters. Cue any row after it vanishes and they get most of it. Did they experience all twelve? This is the "overflow" debate.

More paradigms

  • BlindsightDamage to primary visual cortex (V1). Patients deny seeing in part of the visual field, yet point to and discriminate objects there well above chance.
  • Inattentional blindnessCount basketball passes and you miss a person in a gorilla suit walking through (Simons & Chabris, 1999).
  • Attentional blinkA second target 200–500 ms after a first is often missed entirely. Awareness appears all-or-none here.
  • No-reportInfer the percept from eye movements or pupil size instead of a button press, so report-related activity drops out (Tsuchiya, Koch and colleagues, 2015).
  • Mental imagery"Imagine playing tennis" lights up supplementary motor area; "imagine walking your house" lights up parahippocampal cortex. Used to detect hidden awareness (Owen, 2006).

Instruments

  • fMRIBlood-oxygen signal. Millimetre resolution, but sluggish: the haemodynamic response peaks around 5–6 s.
  • EEG / MEGElectrical / magnetic fields at the scalp. Millisecond timing, centimetre-level blur.
  • iEEGElectrodes inside the skull of epilepsy patients awaiting surgery. Fast and precise, but only where clinicians placed them.
  • TMSA magnetic pulse that briefly perturbs a patch of cortex. Turns correlation into causation, and powers "zap and zip".

Measuring the level: "zap and zip"

Marcello Massimini's group (University of Milan), with Tononi, pulses the cortex with TMS and records the EEG reverberation. A conscious brain answers with a response that is both widespread (integrated) and varied (differentiated). They binarise the significant responses across channels and time, compress them with Lempel–Ziv, and normalise. That's the PCI. In a benchmark population a cut-off of 0.31 separated conscious from unconscious conditions; applied to brain-injured patients, it flagged about 95% of minimally conscious patients as conscious and found high values in some patients diagnosed as unresponsive.

Why it matters: a 2024 multicentre study in the New England Journal of Medicine (Bodien and colleagues) found that roughly a quarter of patients who showed no behavioural response could still follow commands in their brain activity. That's called cognitive motor dissociation.

04 · The map

Front of the brain, or back?

The sharpest empirical fight in the field is geographic. Front theories (workspace, higher-order) say experience needs prefrontal and parietal "higher" areas. Back theories (integrated information, recurrent processing) say posterior sensory cortex is enough, and prefrontal activity mostly reflects thinking about, or reporting, what you experienced.

The report confound drives this. When rivalry is studied with button presses, frontal areas light up; when the percept is read from eye movements instead, much of that frontal activity shrinks (Frässle and colleagues, 2014). Front theorists reply that subtler prefrontal signals remain, and that large frontal lesions are rarely as clean as claimed.

05 · The suspects

Nine answers to one question

Where does the light come from? The answers fall into three camps. Each theory below has a plain summary, then a technical layer: mechanism, signatures, landmark experiments, and its cracks.

Camp 1 · In the brain's wiring

Neuroscience theories that point at specific circuits and timing. These make the most testable predictions.

Global Neuronal Workspace

Conscious = globally broadcast.

The brain runs many specialised processors in parallel, unconsciously. A limited-capacity "workspace" picks one piece of information at a time and broadcasts it to all of them. Being conscious of something just is that information being globally available.

Who
Stanislas Dehaene · Collège de France & NeuroSpin (Saclay, near Paris), with Jean-Pierre Changeux and Lionel Naccache. Cognitive version by Bernard Baars (1988).

Mechanism

"Workspace neurons" are large pyramidal cells with long axons, especially in cortical layers II/III and densest in prefrontal and parietal cortex. They link distant areas in reciprocal loops. When bottom-up evidence for a stimulus crosses a threshold, these loops reinforce each other and the network snaps into a self-sustaining state. Dehaene calls this ignition: nonlinear, all-or-none, and late.

Signatures

  • P3bA large positive EEG wave over parietal scalp from ~300 ms, present for seen and nearly absent for unseen stimuli.
  • Late sustainedContent becomes decodable for longer and in more areas once seen.
  • Long-range syncBeta/gamma-band coherence between distant regions.
  • BimodalityNear threshold, trials split into "ignited" and "not", not a smooth gradient.

Key experiments

  • 2001 · Dehaene et al.Masked words still activate the visual word form area, but only seen words trigger widespread frontal–parietal activation.
  • 2004 · Sergent & DehaeneIn the attentional blink, people rate visibility as either clearly seen or not at all. Bimodal, as ignition predicts.
  • 2017 · Dehaene, Lau & KouiderIn Science, argued machines could be conscious if they had global availability plus self-monitoring.

Cracks

  • Access, not feeling. Broadcast explains availability for report. Critics like Ned Block say phenomenal experience may overflow it, as Sperling's grid suggests.
  • Report confound. Prefrontal ignition may be preparing a response, not experiencing. No-report paradigms weaken frontal signals.
  • 2025 adversarial test. No ignition when stimuli disappeared, and stimulus orientation (clearly experienced) couldn't be decoded from prefrontal cortex. See the showdown.

Integrated Information Theory

Conscious = a physical system whose whole has irreducible cause–effect power.

IIT starts from experience, not the brain. It lists properties every experience has, translates each into a requirement on the physical substrate, and arrives at a quantity, Φ ("phi"), measuring how much a system is more than the sum of its parts. Consciousness is that integrated structure, in brains or anywhere else.

Who
Giulio Tononi · Wisconsin Institute for Sleep and Consciousness, UW–Madison. Champion: Christof Koch · Allen Institute, Seattle, and Tiny Blue Dot Foundation. Current version: IIT 4.0 (2023).

Axioms → postulates

  • 0 · ExistenceExperience exists, here and now. Substrate: must have cause–effect power.
  • IntrinsicalityExperience exists for itself. Power must be on itself, not just for an observer.
  • InformationEach experience is specific. Substrate must select a specific cause–effect state.
  • IntegrationExperience is unified. Cutting the system must lose cause–effect power.
  • ExclusionExperience is definite. Only the grain and borders with maximal Φ count.
  • CompositionExperience is structured. Its distinctions and relations form a "Φ-structure".

The computation, roughly

Φ(S) = distance( CES(S), CES(S cut at MIP) )
CES = cause–effect structure · MIP = minimum information partition

Take the system in its current state. For every possible way of cutting it into parts, compute how much its cause–effect structure is degraded. The cut that hurts least is the minimum information partition; the damage there is Φ. The subset of the system with maximal Φ is the "main complex", which IIT identifies with the conscious subject. The quality of experience is the shape of its Φ-structure.

Signature predictions

  • Hot zoneThe grid-like, recurrent posterior cortex should be the substrate, not prefrontal cortex.
  • CerebellumExplains a real puzzle. See the chart below.
  • Silent neuronsInactive but connected neurons still shape experience. Inactivated ones do not. A strange, testable claim.
  • Feedforward = 0Any purely feedforward system has Φ = 0, whatever its behaviour.

Neuron counts, Azevedo et al. (2009); bars to scale. Losing much of the cerebellum disturbs movement but not experience. IIT's answer: its circuitry is many parallel, weakly interacting modules, so Φ is low despite the neuron count.

Key experiments

  • 2013 · Casali, Massimini et al.The PCI ("zap and zip") index, inspired by IIT, separates conscious from unconscious states across sleep, anaesthesia and brain injury.
  • 2025 · Cogitate ConsortiumPre-registered test against the workspace theory. Key IIT prediction of sustained synchronisation within posterior cortex was not found.
  • 2026 · INTREPIDA second adversarial collaboration pitting IIT against Active Inference (Friston) and Neurorepresentationalism (Pennartz) published its joint review and planned experiments.

Cracks

  • Uncomputable in practice. The number of partitions grows super-exponentially. Exact Φ is feasible only for systems of about a dozen elements. Brain-scale Φ is unreachable, so tests use proxies.
  • The grid objection. Scott Aaronson (2014) showed simple, inactive lattices of logic gates can have enormous Φ. IIT accepts the result: such a grid would be more conscious than you.
  • The unfolding argument. Any recurrent network can be "unfolded" into a feedforward one with identical input–output behaviour (Doerig et al., 2019). Same behaviour, opposite Φ. So no behavioural test can confirm IIT.
  • The pseudoscience fight. In September 2023, 124 researchers signed a letter calling IIT pseudoscience. A revised version ran in Nature Neuroscience in 2025, with a reply from Tononi's team. Many, including Chalmers and Seth, called the label too harsh.

Higher-Order Theories

Conscious = a mental state that is itself represented.

A signal in visual cortex can guide behaviour without being experienced. It becomes conscious only when a higher-order system, mainly in prefrontal cortex, represents it: roughly, "this sensory state is a reliable reflection of the world right now".

Who
Hakwan Lau · Institute for Basic Science, South Korea (joined 2024; previously RIKEN, UCLA, Columbia). Philosopher David Rosenthal (CUNY) built the original higher-order thought theory. Steve Fleming · UCL works on metacognition.

Mechanism: perceptual reality monitoring

Lau's version works like the discriminator in a generative adversarial network. Early sensory areas generate activity from three sources: the world, imagination, and noise. A prefrontal monitor learns to classify each pattern. When it tags a pattern "real, from outside, now", that pattern is experienced as perception. The tagging is automatic, not deliberate reflection.

Signatures

  • meta-d′Signal-detection measure of how well confidence tracks accuracy (Maniscalco & Lau, 2012). Lets you separate "seeing" from "knowing you see".
  • DissociationSame task accuracy, different awareness. The difference should live in prefrontal cortex.

Key experiments

  • 1970s on · WeiskrantzBlindsight patients discriminate stimuli they deny seeing. On this view: first-order vision intact, higher-order tag missing.
  • 2006 · Lau & PassinghamMasking conditions matched for accuracy but differing in reported visibility. Visibility tracked mid-dorsolateral prefrontal activity.
  • 2010 · Rounis et al.TMS over dorsolateral PFC reduced metacognitive sensitivity without hurting accuracy. A later replication attempt failed, a reminder of how fragile these effects are.

Cracks

  • The misrepresentation problem. What if the higher-order state represents a red experience that isn't there? Then either you experience red with no red signal, or the theory needs patching.
  • Why would a tag feel like anything? It relocates the hard problem to the monitor.
  • Frontal lesions. Patients with large prefrontal damage don't obviously lose experience.
  • Animals and infants. If consciousness needs sophisticated self-monitoring, how far down does it go?

Recurrent Processing Theory

Conscious = sensory areas talking back to themselves.

The first wave of visual processing races up the hierarchy and is unconscious, however sophisticated. Experience begins when higher visual areas feed back to lower ones and the loop reverberates. No prefrontal involvement required.

Who
Victor Lamme · University of Amsterdam.

Four stages

  • 1 · Superficial FFFeedforward sweep reaches V1 at ~40–60 ms, higher areas by ~100–150 ms. Features, even categories, extracted. Unconscious.
  • 2 · Deep FFSweep reaches motor and frontal areas; can drive reflex-like responses. Still unconscious.
  • 3 · Local recurrenceFeedback within visual cortex binds features, separates figure from ground. Phenomenal experience, on this theory.
  • 4 · Global recurrenceLoops extend to frontoparietal areas. Access and report. This is where the workspace theory starts counting.

Key experiments

  • 2001 · Supèr, Spekreijse & LammeIn monkeys, V1 neurons fire more for a figure than its background from ~100 ms, a feedback effect. When the monkey failed to report the figure, the modulation was missing even though the feedforward response was intact.
  • Masking studiesA mask arriving at the right moment interrupts recurrence without blocking the feedforward sweep, which is why masked stimuli can still prime responses.

Cracks

  • Unreportable experience. Stage 3 is conscious but may never be reported. By construction, that's very hard to verify.
  • Recurrence is everywhere. Some feedback persists under anaesthesia and in unconscious processing. What's special about the conscious kind?
  • Mechanism, not explanation. Even if it's the right circuit, it doesn't say why looping should feel like something.
Camp 2 · In the brain's models of itself

Theories built on the idea that the brain runs models, of the world, the body and its own processes, and experience is tied to those models.

Predictive Processing & the Beast Machine

Experience is the brain's best guess, rooted in keeping the body alive.

The brain never sees the world directly. It runs a generative model, predicts its sensory input, and updates on the error. What you experience is the prediction, a "controlled hallucination". The deepest predictions are about the inside of the body, and that ties consciousness to being a living organism.

Who
Anil Seth · Sussex Centre for Consciousness Science, University of Sussex, Brighton. Mathematical backbone from Karl Friston · UCL, London (free-energy principle, active inference).

The maths

p(cause | senses) ∝ p(senses | cause) · p(cause)
posterior ∝ likelihood × prior
F = DKL[ q(cause) ‖ p(cause) ] − Eq[ ln p(senses | cause) ] ≥ −ln p(senses)
free energy = complexity − accuracy · an upper bound on surprise

Each cortical level sends predictions down and receives prediction errors up. Errors are weighted by precision (inverse variance): how much the brain trusts that signal right now. Attention, on this view, is precision-weighting. Minimising free energy can happen two ways: change the model (perception) or change the world to fit the model (active inference, i.e. action).

Seth's step

Interoceptive inference. The brain also predicts heartbeat, blood pressure, gut and chemistry, in order to regulate them. Emotions are perceptions of these predictions. The self is a bundle of such predictions. So consciousness is less like a computer and more like a "beast machine": tied to staying alive.

Key experiments

  • Mooney imagesA black-and-white blob is meaningless until you see the original photo. After that you can't un-see it. A prior permanently changed perception.
  • 2017 · Hallucination MachineSeth's lab fed panoramic VR video through a DeepDream-style network to mimic psychedelic visuals, testing "over-weighted priors" as a model of hallucination.
  • Cardiac timingStimuli presented at different points of the heartbeat are perceived differently, supporting body–perception coupling.

Cracks

  • A framework, not a theory. With free choice of priors and precisions, almost any result can be fitted. Hard to falsify.
  • Prediction without experience. Most predictive processing is unconscious, like motor control. What makes some predictions conscious?
  • Sidesteps the hard problem. Seth openly aims at the "real problem": explaining the properties of experience rather than why it exists.

Attention Schema Theory

Awareness is the brain's simplified model of its own attention.

Control engineering says a good controller needs a model of what it controls. The brain controls attention, so it builds a model of attention: the attention schema. Like the body schema, it's a sketch, not a wiring diagram. The sketch describes attention as an invisible inner essence that grabs things. That description is what we report as awareness.

Who
Michael Graziano · Princeton Neuroscience Institute, Princeton University.

Mechanism

  • AttentionBiased competition among signals for limited processing. A physical, mechanistic process.
  • SchemaA predictive, simplified model of that process, proposed to involve the temporoparietal junction and superior temporal sulcus.
  • Use 1Endogenous control of your own attention.
  • Use 2Modelling other people's attention (gaze following, theory of mind). Why we attribute minds to others.

Key experiments

  • 2016 · Webb, Kean & GrazianoWhen a cue is unseen, attention is still drawn to it, but people are worse at controlling that attention. Prediction: awareness = control model.
  • 2021 · Wilterson & GrazianoA reinforcement-learning agent given an attention schema learned to control its visual attention better than one without.

Cracks

  • Explains the claim, not the feeling. It's a theory of the meta-problem. If you think the felt quality is real, AST deflates it rather than explains it.
  • Attention ≠ consciousness. Koch and Tsuchiya (2007) argued each can occur without the other, loosening the tie AST depends on.

Illusionism

Phenomenal consciousness, in the mysterious sense, doesn't exist. It only seems to.

The brain really processes information and really represents itself. But it represents its own states as having ineffable, private "qualia". Those representations are wrong, like the Müller-Lyer lines. Explain why the brain misrepresents itself and nothing is left over.

Who
Daniel Dennett · Tufts University (1942–2024). Keith Frankish · University of Sheffield, who named the view in 2016.

Core moves

  • No theatreDennett rejected the "Cartesian theatre", a single place where it all comes together for a viewer. Yes, that's this page's metaphor. He'd object.
  • Multiple draftsMany parallel content-fixations compete. "Consciousness" is whichever ones win influence: fame in the brain.
  • HeterophenomenologyTreat people's reports as data about what seems to them, not as infallible facts about their inner life.
  • Meta-problemSolve why we're convinced there's a hard problem, and you've solved it.

Supporting evidence

  • Peripheral visionYour visual field feels rich and coloured to the edges. Colour and detail vision actually collapse a few degrees from where you look.
  • Change blindnessLarge changes in a scene go unnoticed across a brief flicker. We see far less than we think.
  • 2005 · Choice blindnessJohansson and Hall swapped photos people had chosen; most didn't notice and confidently justified the choice they never made.

Cracks

  • The illusion problem. An illusion is something seeming a certain way. Seeming is the very thing to be explained.
  • Too costly. Philosopher Galen Strawson called denying experience the silliest claim ever made. Many find pain-as-illusion unacceptable.
Camp 3 · Beyond ordinary neurons

Views holding that standard neuroscience can't get there alone: either new physics is needed, or consciousness is built into matter itself.

Orchestrated Objective Reduction

Moments of awareness are quantum-state collapses inside neurons.

Penrose argued, from Gödel's incompleteness theorem, that human understanding isn't computable, so no algorithm (and no ordinary neural network) can explain it. He proposed a new physical process. Hameroff supplied the place: microtubules, the protein scaffolding inside every neuron.

Who
Sir Roger Penrose · Mathematical Institute, University of Oxford (Nobel Prize in Physics, 2020, for black-hole work). Stuart Hameroff · anaesthesiologist, Center for Consciousness Studies, University of Arizona, Tucson.

Mechanism

τ ≈ ħ / EG
a superposition self-collapses after time τ · EG = gravitational self-energy of the mass separation

In ordinary quantum mechanics, superpositions collapse on measurement, and nobody agrees what that means. Penrose proposes objective reduction: a superposition of different mass arrangements becomes unstable in spacetime and collapses on its own after τ. Hameroff proposes tubulin proteins enter superpositions, "orchestrated" by synaptic inputs. Each collapse is a discrete conscious moment, roughly every 25 ms, matching 40 Hz gamma rhythms.

The decoherence fight, to scale

Max Tegmark calculated that microtubule superpositions would decohere in 10⁻²⁰ to 10⁻¹³ s, around ten orders of magnitude too fast. Hameroff's group recalculated 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ s with shielding assumptions. Orch OR needs ~25 ms.

Key experiments

  • 2022 · Gran Sasso, ItalyCatalina Curceanu's team used an underground radiation experiment to bound gravity-related collapse. The simplest Diósi–Penrose model came out highly implausible for Orch OR; more complex variants survive.
  • 2024 · Anaesthesia in ratsA drug that stabilises microtubules delayed loss of consciousness under isoflurane by about a minute. Supporters read it as a microtubule role in anaesthesia; it doesn't show quantum effects.

Cracks

  • Warm, wet and noisy. Decoherence estimates above. The brain is a terrible place to keep a quantum state.
  • The Gödel argument. Most logicians reject the step from incompleteness to non-computable minds.
  • Explanatory gap stays. Even if collapses happen, why would a collapse feel like anything?

Panpsychism

Experience is a fundamental feature of matter.

Physics tells us what matter does: mass, charge, how things push and pull. It's silent on what matter is intrinsically. Panpsychists propose that intrinsic nature is experiential. Electrons have something unimaginably simple; brains organise vast amounts of it into a mind.

Who
Philip Goff · Durham University. Roots in Bertrand Russell and Arthur Eddington (1920s). Koch is sympathetic; IIT has a panpsychist streak.

The argument (Russellian monism)

  • Premise 1Physics describes only structure and dispositions, not intrinsic natures.
  • Premise 2Dispositions need something to be dispositions of.
  • Premise 3The only intrinsic nature we know directly is our own experience.
  • ConclusionSimplest hypothesis: matter's intrinsic nature is experiential, everywhere.

Cracks

  • The combination problem. William James (1890): take twelve people, give each one word of a sentence, and nowhere is the whole sentence experienced. How do micro-subjects sum into one subject? Goff has proposed "phenomenal bonding"; no answer is widely accepted.
  • Untestable. Panpsychism makes no prediction a brain scan could check. It reframes the hard problem rather than solving it.
  • Why not rocks? It must explain why some aggregates (brains) combine into minds and others (rocks) don't. That's the same question neuroscience faces.
06 · The showdown

Rivals in one ring

The Cogitate Consortium brought theory proponents and theory-neutral labs together. Before collecting data, both camps signed off on the design, their divergent predictions, and what results would count against them. An approach first championed by Daniel Kahneman. Seven years later it appeared in Nature (online 30 April 2025).

Design

  • People256 participants across fMRI, MEG and intracranial EEG (epilepsy patients), in several labs.
  • StimuliClearly visible faces, objects, letters and false fonts at different orientations, shown for 0.5, 1.0 or 1.5 s. Some were task targets, most weren't, so report could be separated from experience.
  • LogicBoth theories agree these stimuli are consciously seen. They disagree on where, when and how connected the conscious content should be.

Predictions and results

QuestionIIT predictedGNWT predictedFound
Where is conscious content?Posterior cortexPrefrontal cortex holds all content featuresContent in visual, ventrotemporal and inferior frontal cortex. Orientation not decodable in PFC: a hit to GNWT.
When: how does it track duration?Sustained posterior activity for as long as the stimulus is seenIgnition at onset and again at offset; nothing sustained neededSustained responses in occipital/lateral temporal cortex tracked duration: partial IIT support. Offset ignition largely absent.
ConnectivitySustained synchronisation within posterior cortexLong-range frontal–posterior synchrony at ignitionPosterior sync not found: a hit to IIT. Content-specific frontal–early visual sync found.

The authors also flagged a field-wide issue: support for a theory often depended on the analysis choices made, a sign of confirmation bias that pre-registration is meant to fix.

IIT
Got its "where" and partly its "when". Lost its core connectivity claim.
Workspace
Got onset ignition. Lost offset ignition and full prefrontal content.

Fallout followed. Media coverage of the 2023 preliminary results called IIT a leading, tested theory, which helped prompt the 124-signature pseudoscience letter. The field's response was more adversarial collaborations, not fewer: INTREPID now pits IIT against Friston's active inference and Pennartz's neurorepresentationalism, with a joint review published in 2026 laying out planned multi-site experiments.

07 · Why it matters now

Could an AI feel?

In 2023, 19 researchers including Patrick Butlin, Robert Long and Yoshua Bengio turned five of these theories (recurrent processing, global workspace, higher-order, predictive processing, attention schema) into a checklist of computational "indicator properties", then audited AI systems. Their conclusion: no current system is a strong candidate, but no obvious technical barrier stops building one that ticks the boxes. IIT was left out because it denies that computation alone is enough.

10 · Glossary

Terms of art

Qualia
The felt qualities of experience: the redness of red, the hurt of pain.
NCC
Neural correlate of consciousness: minimal neural mechanisms jointly sufficient for a specific percept.
Contrastive method
Compare seen vs unseen with the stimulus held constant.
Report confound
Brain activity caused by deciding and responding, mistaken for activity of experiencing.
Ignition
GNWT's sudden, self-sustaining, brain-wide activation when content becomes conscious.
P3b
Late positive EEG wave (~300 ms+) linked to conscious access.
Φ (phi)
IIT's measure of integrated information: cause–effect power lost under the least damaging cut.
MIP
Minimum information partition: the cut of a system that loses least.
PCI
Perturbational complexity index: compressibility of the brain's response to a TMS pulse.
meta-d′
How well a person's confidence tracks whether they were right. A measure of metacognition.
Precision
In predictive processing, inverse variance: how much a signal is trusted.
Free energy
An upper bound on surprise that organisms are said to minimise by perceiving and acting.
Decoherence
Loss of a quantum superposition through interaction with the environment.
Overflow
The claim that we experience more than we can access or report.
UWS
Unresponsive wakefulness syndrome: eyes open, no reliable sign of awareness.
Cognitive motor dissociation
Following commands in brain activity while unable to show it in behaviour.
Where it stands

Nobody has found the switch.

The front-versus-back fight is unresolved, the deepest theories are the hardest to test, and the most testable ones may only explain access, not feeling. What has changed is method: theories now commit to predictions in advance and accept the score.
