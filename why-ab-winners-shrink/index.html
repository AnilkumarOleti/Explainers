Why winners shrink

A test is a wobbly measurement

An A/B test shows the old page to half your visitors and a new idea to the other half, then compares what they buy. The two halves are never the same people, so the answer wobbles.

To clear the line an idea must be good, lucky, or both. So the winners are a lucky crowd.

Two names for that picture

The winner's curse

Pick the best of many wobbly numbers and you pick the one luck pushed highest. The name comes from 1971 oil-field auctions, where the company that bid most was usually the one that had guessed too high.

Regression to the mean

Measure again and the luck is fresh. Extreme results drift back toward ordinary. In 1886 Francis Galton found that very tall parents had tall children, though about a third closer to average than they were.

The curse is what picking does. Regression is what you see on the second look.

Don't add up your wins

Ten launches that each tested at +4% did not grow the business by 40%.

Sometimes the shrink is real. Novelty wears off. Seasons change. Launch reaches people the test never saw. The bounce needs none of that. It vanishes without anything changing, because it was never there.

How the pin board is set up. A typical idea is really worth about ±1%. An idea launches when its result beats 1.96 times the test's wobble, the usual "p < 0.05" bar. Test size ×1 gives a wobble of ±1.5%, which is roughly 170,000 visitors per version on a page where 5% of them buy. The trim uses the classic shrinkage rule: real spread² ÷ (real spread² + wobble²). In a world with bigger ideas, the bounce matters less.
