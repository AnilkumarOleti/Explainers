Key events of WW-II

The war had one shape. Axis power rose for nearly three years, turned in eight months, and drained away over the next two and a half.

Before the map · 1931 to 1939

Three empires in a hurry

Germany, Italy and Japan each wanted more land and were willing to fight for it. Together they were called the Axis. The countries that fought them were the Allies.

Axis

  • Germany led by Hitler
  • Italy until 1943
  • Japan in China since 1937

Allies

  • Britain and its empire from 1939
  • France from 1939
  • China fighting Japan since 1937
  • Soviet Union from 1941
  • United States from 1941

Germany wanted to undo its defeat in the First World War and take land in the east. Japan wanted the resources of China and south-east Asia. Through the 1930s Britain and France gave way to avoid another war. When Germany invaded Poland in September 1939, they stopped giving way.

Act I · Sep 1939 to May 1942 · 33 months

The Flood

The Axis attacks outward in every direction and almost nothing stops it.

Arrows show the direction of each attack. They are not drawn to geographic scale.

11 Sep 1939Europe

Germany invades Poland

Britain and France declare war two days later. Poland is overrun in five weeks.

2May–Jun 1940Europe

France falls

German tanks reach the Channel in ten days. About 338,000 Allied troops escape by sea from Dunkirk, and France surrenders on 22 June.

3Jul–Oct 1940Europe

Battle of Britain

The Royal Air Force beats off the German air force, and Hitler shelves his invasion of Britain. It is his first defeat.

422 Jun 1941Eastern Front

Germany invades the Soviet Union

Three million troops attack along a 3,000 km front and are stopped outside Moscow in December. The deadliest front of the war opens.

57 Dec 1941Pacific

Pearl Harbor

Japan bombs the US fleet in Hawaii. The United States joins the war, and two separate wars become one.

620 Jan 1942Occupied Europe

The Holocaust

Mass shootings began in 1941. At Wannsee, Nazi officials now coordinate the murder of all of Europe's Jews. Six million are killed by 1945.

715 Feb 1942South-east Asia

Singapore falls

About 80,000 British, Indian and Australian troops surrender. By May, Japan holds south-east Asia from Burma to the Philippines.

Act II · Jun 1942 to Feb 1943 · 8 months

The Turn

Three battles, on sea, sand and snow, stop the Axis for good.

84–7 Jun 1942Pacific

Midway

US codebreakers set a trap. Japan loses four aircraft carriers in one battle and goes on the defensive.

9Oct–Nov 1942North Africa

El Alamein

Britain's Eighth Army breaks the German and Italian army in the Egyptian desert. The Axis is out of Africa by May 1943.

10Aug 1942–Feb 1943Eastern Front

Stalingrad

Soviet armies surround an entire German army in the city. Its 91,000 survivors surrender, and Germany never regains the upper hand in the east.

Act III · Feb 1943 to Sep 1945 · 31 months

The Ebb

The arrows reverse. The Allies close in on Germany and Japan from every side.

Arrows show which side each attack came from. They are not drawn to geographic scale.

11Jul 1943Eastern Front · Italy

Kursk and Sicily

Germany's last big attack in the east fails at Kursk. The same week the Allies land in Sicily, and Italy surrenders in September.

12Mar–Jul 1944India–Burma border

Imphal and Kohima

Japan invades north-east India. A largely Indian army holds both towns and hands Japan its worst land defeat so far.

136 Jun 1944France

D-Day

About 156,000 Allied troops land in Normandy, the largest invasion by sea in history. Paris is free by August.

1422 Jun 1944Eastern Front

Operation Bagration

The Red Army destroys Germany's central army group in Belarus and advances about 500 km in five weeks.

1523–26 Oct 1944Philippines

Leyte Gulf

The largest naval battle of the war. Japan's navy is finished as a fighting force.

16Dec 1944–Jan 1945Belgium

Battle of the Bulge

Germany's last attack in the west runs out of fuel in the Ardennes forest. Its final reserves are spent.

178 May 1945Europe

Germany surrenders

Soviet troops take Berlin. Hitler kills himself on 30 April, and Germany surrenders a week later.

186 and 9 Aug 1945Japan

Hiroshima and Nagasaki

The United States drops two atomic bombs, killing an estimated 150,000 to 250,000 people. Between the two, the Soviet Union attacks Japan in Manchuria.

192 Sep 1945Tokyo Bay

Japan surrenders

The emperor announces the surrender on 15 August. It is signed six years and one day after the war began.

After the tide · 1945 onward

What it left behind

Most of the world we live in was arranged in the five years after the surrender.

  • 1945

    The United Nations

    Fifty countries sign a charter meant to stop a third world war.

  • 1945–1991

    Two superpowers

    The United States and the Soviet Union divide Europe and begin the Cold War.

  • 1945

    The nuclear age

    One bomb can now destroy a city. Every later war is fought under that shadow.

  • 1947 onward

    The end of empires

    Exhausted European powers let go. India and Pakistan become independent in 1947, and most of Asia and Africa follow.

  • 1945–1948

    Law for war crimes

    Nazi leaders are tried at Nuremberg. Genocide becomes a crime in international law.

  • 1944

    A new money system

    The World Bank and the IMF are set up at Bretton Woods to rebuild trade.
