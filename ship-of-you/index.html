How do we know? Nuclear bomb tests flooded the air with carbon-14. Every cell built in those years carries a dated stamp. Scientists read the stamp to find each cell’s age.

Some atoms truly stay: tooth enamel, the centre of your eye lens, much of the DNA in your brain cells.

“But my neurons stay!”

The cell stays. Its parts don’t.

Most proteins inside a neuron are rebuilt within days to weeks. Its connections keep rewiring too. Same cell, new contents.

The big idea

You are the swirl, not the water.

Follow the orange drop. It is gone in seconds. The swirl stays, because of how the water moves. Swap water for atoms, and that is you.

Why yesterday’s you is still you

No single thread runs the whole way.

Age 5Age 20Age 40Today

Today-you shares almost everything with yesterday-you. Memories, habits, body. That overlap, day after day, makes one rope.

So, what is you?

Three answers

Ruled out

Your stuff

The atoms. They keep leaving.

A living process

Your body, alive

The organism that keeps rebuilding itself. A swirl of matter.

A pattern

Your mind’s thread

Memories, habits, personality, passed from day to day.

Thinkers still argue between the last two. But almost all agree: you are a pattern that keeps going, not a pile of parts.

The hard case

What if there were two of you?

A machine copies you perfectly. Both remember your childhood. Which one is you?

Philosopher Derek Parfit said there may be no hidden fact to find. What matters is the connection, and both have it.

You are not a thing.
You are something happening.
