One second inside you

One heartbeat, followed from your whole body down to a spinning motor a hundred-thousandth of a millimetre wide.

This page lasts exactly one second. Scroll, and the clock runs.

Lub

Your heart’s inlet valves snap shut. The squeeze begins.

70 ml

Pushed out of the left side in one squeeze, about 60% of what it holds. The right side sends another 70 ml to your lungs.

≈ 6,000 litres a day

At one beat a second. About 30 bathtubs, every day of your life.

Dub

The outlet valves close. For the rest of this second the heart relaxes and refills. Its own arteries fill mostly now, between squeezes.

Into the wall

Arteries split again and again, down to capillaries a tenth the width of a hair. Every heart muscle cell has one close by.

Meanwhile, in your head

≈ 25 billion spikes

From 86 billion neurons. Firing costs energy, so the average neuron fires less than once a second.

Estimates run from about 10 to 170 billion spikes a second.

Single file

Red blood cells squeeze through capillaries one at a time, handing oxygen to the muscle next door.

3.8 million cells

Replaced across your body in this second. About 2 million of them are new red blood cells.

Blood 86%Gut lining 12%Everything else 2%

Heart muscle is the exception. About 1% of it renews each year at age 25, so you keep most of yours for life.

Inside one heart cell

Striped muscle fibres, with power plants packed in rows between them. In a human heart cell, mitochondria fill about 28% of the space.

The power plant

Oxygen’s trip ends here. A mitochondrion uses it to burn food: not with fire, but by pumping protons into its folds, charging them like a battery.

About 0.15 volts across a membrane 5 nanometres thick. That’s 30 million volts per metre, ten times what makes air spark.

Pumps and turbines

Pumps on the flat walls push protons (H⁺) into each fold. The only easy way back out is through the turbines lining its edge.

ATP synthase

A real rotary motor. Protons flowing through spin the rotor, and the head turns that spin into ATP.

  • 8protons per turn
  • 3ATP per turn
  • ~100turns a second

Shown about 250 times slower than real.

≈ 600 billion billion ATP

Made across your body this second, sitting still. ATP is the energy coin: cells spend it by snapping off the last phosphate, then recharge it.

About 40 kg a day at rest, more when you move. You hold only 100 to 250 g at once, so each molecule is recharged hundreds of times a day.

Where the numbers come from

  1. 70 ml per beat, from about 120 ml when full: StatPearls, Physiology, Stroke Volume.
  2. 330 billion cells replaced a day (3.8 million a second), almost 90% blood cells, about 12% gut lining: Sender & Milo, Nature Medicine 2021, summarised by the Weizmann Institute.
  3. About 2 million new red blood cells a second: MedlinePlus.
  4. Heart muscle renews about 1% a year at 25, 0.45% at 75: Bergmann et al., Science 2009.
  5. 86 billion neurons: Azevedo et al., 2009. Average firing of roughly 0.1 to 2 spikes a second, around 0.3 brain-wide: AI Impacts, from Lennie 2003.
  6. Mitochondria fill 27.9% of a human heart muscle cell: Sulkin et al., 2014.
  7. An 8-part rotor, 3 ATP per turn, about 100 turns a second: Watt et al., PNAS 2010.
  8. Turbines in rows along the fold edges, pumps on the flat walls: Davies et al., PNAS 2011.
  9. About your body weight in ATP each day: BioNumbers. The per-second figure is our arithmetic: about 250 ml of oxygen a minute at rest is 1.1×10²⁰ O₂ molecules a second, and each makes about 5 ATP.
  10. Heat: the same oxygen releases about 20 joules per millilitre. Membrane field: 0.15 V across 5 nm is 3×10⁷ V/m; air breaks down near 3×10⁶ V/m.
