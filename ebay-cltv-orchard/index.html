A field guide to customer lifetime value for eBay analytics

What is an eBay customer worth?

Picture every customer as a tree you planted. Each season a living tree gives fruit. Some die young, a few grow into giants, and fruit picked years from now is worth less than fruit picked today.

Customer lifetime value (CLTV) is the harvest you expect from one planted tree, counted in today's money.

Plot 01Why CLTV exists

The first harvest rarely pays for the sapling

You pay to acquire a customer today: ads, coupons, sales time. They pay you back slowly, one order at a time, for as long as they stay.

CLTV puts a number on that slow payback, so you can answer the question every growth team asks: how much can we afford to spend to win one customer?

Rule of thumb

SaaS investors popularised LTV at least 3 × CAC, payback under 12 months. Treat it as a smell test. The ratio swings with the horizon you choose and with whether LTV is measured in revenue or margin. Stretch the horizon above and watch it move.

Which lever moves CLTV most from here?

  • Use margin, not revenue. For a marketplace that means take rate, ads and fees minus incentives and costs, never GMV.
  • Retention compounds. The higher retention already is, the more each extra point is worth. Push retention toward 95% and watch the sensitivity bars.
  • Fix the horizon and discount rate first. Many teams use 3 to 5 years and their cost of capital. Two CLTVs with different conventions can't be compared.
Go deeper: the margin multiple

Gupta and Lehmann (2003) write the constant case with margin arriving at the end of each period: CLTV = m · r / (1 + i − r). The ratio r / (1 + i − r) is a margin multiple. At r = 80% and i = 12%, a customer is worth 2.5 times one period's margin. At 90% retention the multiple is 4.1.

The formula gives every customer the same r. The next two chapters show what that assumption hides.

  • Budget with the mean, target with the shape. Totals need the mean. Deciding who to prioritise needs the distribution.
  • Heavy tails break squared-error models. A few whales dominate the loss. Use log targets or zero-inflated losses such as ZILN, and judge models on decile calibration and Gini.
Go deeper: the model behind these charts

Returning customers have lognormal value with spread σ; the never-return group holds zero future value. The Lorenz curve plots the share of total value held by the bottom p% of customers. The Gini coefficient is twice the area between that curve and the diagonal: 0 means everyone is equal, 1 means one customer holds everything.

Plot 04Cohort sorting

The survivor illusion

Plant hardy and fragile saplings together. The fragile ones die first, so each season the surviving orchard is tougher and a larger share of it survives. No single tree changed. The mix did.

That is why cohort retention usually rises with age, and why projecting first-year retention forever undervalues a cohort.

CLTV here uses $100 margin per year, a 10% discount rate and a 40-year horizon.

Retention rate in each year

Models built for this expect heterogeneity. The shifted-beta-geometric (sBG) model gives each customer their own churn probability, drawn from a beta distribution. Fader and Hardie (2007) show that fitting it to the first several periods projects later retention far better than simple curve fits.

Go deeper: sBG in one line

If churn probabilities follow Beta(α, β), retention in period t is rt = (β + t − 1) / (α + β + t − 1). It rises with t for the same sorting reason you just saw. Fit α and β to observed survival by maximum likelihood; with continuous heterogeneity the curve flattens smoothly instead of in two steps.

Plot 05Non-contractual churn

Dead or dormant?

A subscriber cancels and you know it. A marketplace buyer just stops buying, and silence can mean gone for good or just a long gap. Most e-commerce and marketplace CLTV lives in this second world.

Is this buyer still alive?

The BG/NBD model reads three numbers per customer: how many repeat purchases (frequency), when the last one happened (recency) and how long you've known them. Try the examples, drag the sliders, or tap a cell in the heatmap.

Model parameters were fitted on the CDNOW sample of a 1990s online music retailer (Fader, Hardie and Lee, 2005), with time in weeks and money in US dollars. Real marketplace parameters differ; the logic transfers.

  • Silence means different things. Ten purchases then 30 quiet weeks: probably gone. Two purchases then the same 30 quiet weeks: probably just an occasional buyer.
  • One big order doesn't make a big customer. The Gamma-Gamma model pulls a customer's average order toward the population average, and trusts their own average more as orders add up. It assumes order value is independent of frequency, so check that correlation first.
  • Know the quirks. BG/NBD lets customers drop out only right after a purchase, so anyone with no repeat purchase shows P(alive) = 1. Pareto/NBD and the modified BG/NBD avoid this.
Go deeper: the story and the formulas

While alive, a customer buys at a Poisson rate λ, and rates vary across customers as Gamma(r, α). After each purchase they drop out with probability p, which varies as Beta(a, b). Given x repeat purchases, last purchase at tx and age T:

P(alive) = 1 / [ 1 + a/(b+x−1) · ((α+T)/(α+tx))r+x ]   for x > 0

Expected purchases in the next t weeks need a Gaussian hypergeometric function (Fader, Hardie and Lee, 2005, equation 10). Gamma-Gamma's expected order value is p(γ + x·m̄) / (px + q − 1), where m̄ is the average repeat order. Parameters here: r = 0.243, α = 4.414, a = 0.793, b = 2.426; p = 6.25, q = 3.74, γ = 15.44.

In Python, lifetimes (now in maintenance mode) and PyMC-Marketing implement both models; the PyMC versions add uncertainty intervals.

Evaluating a pLTV model

Compare predicted and actual value by decile (calibration), score the ranking with a Gini-style metric, and check the portfolio total. RMSE alone is dominated by a few whales.

The label-lag trap

A 12-month pLTV model needs customers acquired at least 12 months ago. It learns from last year's customers, acquired through last year's channels, so it needs monitoring as the mix moves.

          Rare eventsFrequency is measured in years, so discounting and churn eat most of the future.
          Value is set outsideA device's worth follows resale prices and gold, copper and palladium prices.

          The unit is fuzzyIs the customer the person, the household, the device or the company account?
          Some items lose moneyBulky or hazardous items can cost more to collect and treat than they return.

          Does CLTV make sense? A read by region

          Fit depends more on which customer you mean than on which country. Region moves the dial through regulation, informal competition and how observable customers are. Tap a cell for the reasoning.

          • Strong: repeat relationship, observable data, stable value

          What to keep in mind

          1. Name the unit before you model. Household, business account, producer or device. Each has its own lifetime.
          2. Separate engagement from value. Model whether the customer comes back apart from what the next device will be worth, then stress-test value against commodity and resale prices.
          3. For households, model events, not lifetimes. P(next disposal comes to us) × value per event, with survival curves on replacement cycles. Most of the value is usually the first pickup.
          4. Put CLTV to work in B2B. IT asset disposition contracts, bulk consumers and EPR clients renew and repeat; account-level or contractual models fit them.
          5. Treat regulation as a scenario input. EPR targets (India: 60% to 80%), certificate price bands, take-back mandates and subsidies.
          6. Model density. Pickup cost per device depends on route density, so CLTV varies by city tier and pin code.
          7. Mind the calendar. Festive exchange offers such as Diwali sales, phone launch seasons and subsidy windows move volumes.
          8. Trust drives repeat use. Certified data wiping and transparent pricing are retention levers; track them.
          9. Report ranges. Long cycles make point estimates fragile.

          Buyers

          Non-contractual and heavy-tailed: a small enthusiast group carries a large share of activity.

          • Pay for items (GMV)
          • Pay a buyer protection fee on UK private-seller items
          • Their demand is what makes listing worthwhile

          eBay

          Earns a slice of each sale plus ads, and spends on incentives, protection and marketing.

          • Revenue ≈ 13.8% of GMV (Q3 2026)
          • Value of a customer = margin they generate directly + what they cause others to generate

          Sellers

          C2C private sellers clear out episodically; B2C businesses list continuously, closer to a contract.

          • Pay final value fees (B2C everywhere, C2C in some markets)
          • Buy promoted listings
          • Sellers buy too: selling funds the next purchase

          Example A · Two buyers, the same $600

          Both spent $600 on eBay in the last 48 weeks. Their futures look nothing alike. Frequency and recency say who is likely to keep buying, and Gamma-Gamma refuses to believe one large order describes the next one. Load either buyer into the dead-or-dormant model.

          Buyer A · car parts

          Two orders of $300, the second one 40 weeks ago.

          Buyer B · trading cards

          Thirty orders of about $20, the latest two weeks ago.

          First-purchase category is one of the strongest early signals in a marketplace. A trading-card buyer joins a hobby with weekly drops and live breaks; a buyer of a $300 part may not need another for years. For CRM, a long silence from Buyer B is alarming, while the same silence from Buyer A is normal.

          Predictions use the published CDNOW parameters from chapter 5, so treat them as directional, not as eBay estimates.

          Example B · What is a C2C seller worth when selling is free?

          Germany made private selling essentially free on 1 March 2023. Within a year private listings rose 20% and eBay attracted twice as many new sellers. The UK removed private-seller fees in October 2024, then added a buyer protection fee on 4 February 2025: £0.75 plus 4% of the price up to £300 and 2% from £300 to £4,000.

          If seller LTV counts only seller fees, a free private seller is worth almost nothing, and you would stop acquiring the people who make the marketplace worth visiting.

          Indirect value is monetised at the 13.8% revenue-to-GMV ratio, with a 10% discount rate. Seller fees include $0.40 per order. The buyer-pays model applies the UK formula in the same currency units. Every input is an assumption to replace with experiment results.

          The green and brown segments are where the argument lives. In Germany, eBay reported that users who both buy and sell spend more than twice as much as buy-only users. Part of that gap is selection, since enthusiasts do both. Only a holdout or geo experiment tells you how much selling causes.

          Example C · One global model, many local truths

          Does CLTV make sense in every eBay market? Yes, but with different confidence. The last card sums it up.

          United States

          Largest market: $12.5B Q3 GMV, up 27% (22% excluding acquisitions). Company-wide growth is led by collectibles, Motors and vehicles, with Live scaling fast.

          For CLTV

          Deepest history, so individual pLTV works best here. New formats bring cohorts unlike the past: tag them rather than pooling blindly.

          United Kingdom

          Private sellers fee-free since October 2024; buyer protection fee since 4 February 2025. C2C growing double digits.

          For CLTV

          Two regime breaks in five months. Fee-based seller LTV is near zero for private sellers, so buyer fees and liquidity carry the value. Flag the breaks in training data.

          Germany

          Private selling essentially free since 1 March 2023. New sellers doubled; private listings up 20% in a year.

          For CLTV

          The buy-and-sell loop is a core value driver. Estimate the causal share with experiments before crediting sellers with buyer spend.

          Australia

          C2C growing double digits in Q3 2026.

          For CLTV

          A smaller base means noisier cohorts. Pool with other markets in a hierarchical model and let local data pull the estimate.

          France and Italy

          Fee-free private selling, buyer protection and simpler shipping are expanding, per eBay's Q3 2026 update.

          For CLTV

          Short history under the new rules. Borrow priors from the UK and Germany, re-fit as cohorts mature, keep horizons short.

          Cross-border trade

          eBay International Shipping launched in the UK and Germany; UK buyers pay up to 30% less than under the old Global Shipping Program.

          For CLTV

          Model in local currency, report FX-neutral. Shipping costs and duties change repeat behaviour by trade corridor.

          Depop and Tise

          Depop adds 11M active buyers and 5M active sellers in younger, fashion-led C2C.

          For CLTV

          Separate apps, separate identities. Resolve identities before counting shared customers; fit separate curves first and merge later.

          So, by region?

          Strong where history is long and rules are stable: US buyers, UK and German buyers.

          With care

          Pool and shorten horizons where rules just changed or the base is small: UK and German private sellers, Australia, France and Italy. Keep acquired apps separate until identities are resolved.

          Example D · Marketing and CRM

          • Paid acquisition. Send early pLTV, not first-order GMV, to bidding platforms. Check calibration by decile and keep holdout geos to measure incrementality.
          • Reactivation timing. Use P(alive) rather than a fixed 12-month inactivity rule. A frequent buyer's silence is an earlier, stronger signal.
          • Coupons. Rank by uplift × value. Many high-value enthusiasts are sure things who would buy anyway.
          • Concentration. With 16M enthusiasts among 136M buyers, watch decile shares and the top tail, not just averages.
          • Feedback loops. When pLTV drives bids, cohort mix shifts. Monitor new-cohort quality as a standing metric.

          Example E · Recommerce links the two orchards

          eBay reports that pre-loved and refurbished sales avoided 1.6 million tonnes of CO₂e and 70,000 tonnes of waste in 2025. Refurbished-device buyers behave like strong e-commerce customers. Sellers of used electronics behave like the e-waste households in chapter 10: rare, cycle-driven and price-sensitive. Resell prompts in purchase history connect them, because today's buyer is a seller in a few years.

          Glossary and sources

          CLTV, CLV, LTV
          Expected future value of a customer, usually in contribution margin, discounted to today.
          pLTV
          Predicted LTV for one customer, often made early (day 1 to 30) for bidding and CRM.
          CAC
          Cost to acquire one customer: media, incentives, sales effort.
          Payback period
          Time until cumulative margin repays CAC.
          Contribution margin
          Revenue minus the variable costs a customer causes: fees, shipping subsidies, payments, returns, incentives.
          GMV
          Gross merchandise volume: the total value of goods sold through a marketplace.
          Take rate
          Marketplace revenue as a share of GMV.
          Retention and survival
          Retention rt is period to period; survival S(t) is the share still active after t periods.
          P(alive)
          In non-contractual models, the probability a customer has not silently churned.
          Cohort
          Customers grouped by when or how they were acquired.
          Censoring
          Unfinished histories: a young cohort's future isn't observed yet.
          Discount rate
          The rate that converts future money into today's money, usually the cost of capital.
          Uplift
          The change in behaviour caused by an action, such as a coupon, compared with no action.
          BG/NBD, Pareto/NBD
          Buy-till-you-die models of purchase frequency and silent dropout.
          Gamma-Gamma
          A model of average order value that shrinks individual averages toward the population average.
          sBG
          Shifted-beta-geometric model of contractual retention with heterogeneous churn.
          ZILN
          Zero-inflated lognormal loss for training pLTV models on heavy-tailed, zero-heavy targets.
          EPR
          Extended producer responsibility: producers pay for collecting and recycling what they sell.
          WEEE
          The EU's Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment Directive, and the waste it covers.
          ITAD
          IT asset disposition: retiring, wiping, reselling or recycling business devices.
          Recommerce
          Buying and selling pre-owned or refurbished goods.
