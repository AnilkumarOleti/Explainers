Formula 1 · The pit stop

Two seconds. Four tyres. Twenty people.

Mid-race, a car darts into its garage for fresh tyres. Press play.

Car stopped0.00sec
Wheel gun: undoes one nut Pulls old tyre off Pushes new tyre on Jacks & steadiers lift the car

Three people per wheel. Tyres only: no fuel. Refuelling has been banned since 2010, so cars start with enough for the whole race.

Why stop?

Tyres wear out.

Rubber gets used up, like an eraser. Worn tyres grip less, so every lap gets a little slower. Fresh tyres reset the clock.

Softer tyre = faster, but dies sooner.

Rule

In a dry race, every car must use two different dry tyres. So everyone stops at least once.

The cost

The stop is quick. The detour isn't.

In the pit lane cars must crawl at 80 km/h. Out on track, rivals keep flying at 300.

The payoff

A pit stop is a trade.

Lose ~22 seconds now. Win them back, lap by lap, on fresh tyres while your rival's get worse.

Timing

When you stop matters.

The undercut: stop first, and use your fresh tyres to jump the car ahead.

Safety car = discount stop.

After a crash, everyone follows a safety car slowly. The detour costs far less, so the whole field rushes in.

Stop too early and you run out of tyre. Too late and you crawl. Races are won in between.
