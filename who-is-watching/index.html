About Nisargadatta Maharaj

Born Maruti Shivrampant Kambli (1897–1981), he sold bidis from a small shop in Khetwadi, Mumbai. In 1933 he met his teacher, Siddharameshwar Maharaj of the Inchegeri lineage, who told him to attend to the bare sense “I am”. His talks, given in Marathi and translated by Maurice Frydman as I Am That (1973), made him known worldwide. The practice here paraphrases his approach in plain words; it doesn’t quote his books.

About the pictures

The brain is a see-through side view, front to the left, like the “glass brain” plots in imaging papers: regions on the brain’s inner face show through the outer one. It is schematic. Regions sit roughly where they are, and brightness shows what the cited studies typically report for that kind of moment, not a reading of your brain.

The background is eigengrau, the dark grey people report seeing in total darkness.

Going further

Thomas Metzinger’s The Elephant and the Blind (2024) gathers more than 500 meditators’ reports of “pure awareness”, from a survey spanning 57 countries, and treats them as data.

If it gets unsettling

Self-inquiry can now and then feel strange or unmooring. If it does, open your eyes, feel your feet on the floor, and take a break. If the feeling lingers, talk to someone you trust or to a professional.
