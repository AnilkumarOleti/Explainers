How noise-cancelling headphones work

To make quiet, add a second sound.

The headphones play the exact opposite of the noise around you. Your eardrum gets both at once, and the two add up to almost nothing.

What noise is

Sound is air being pushed and pulled

An engine squeezes the air, then stretches it, hundreds of times a second. Your eardrum rides that pressure up and down. Still air is silence.

Top: air particles · Bottom: the pressure they make
The trick

A push and a pull at the same moment cancel

Pressures add up like numbers. Plus one and minus one leave zero. So the speaker pulls on the air at the exact moment the noise pushes it.

The machine

Inside the cup: listen, flip, play, check

  1. Outer microphoneHears the noise a moment before your ear does.
  2. ChipFlips it. Every push becomes a pull of the same size.
  3. SpeakerPlays the flipped copy along with your music.
  4. Inner microphoneListens beside your ear and corrects whatever is left.

Less than a ten-thousandth of a secondThat is how long sound takes to cross the last 3 cm to your ear, and the anti-noise has to be waiting when it arrives.

The limit

Low rumbles vanish. Sharp sounds get through.

The anti-noise is always a hair late. A low note is a long, slow wave, so a hair late is still nearly on time. A high note is short and fast, and the same delay throws the mirror out of step.

The catch

The noise is still there

The cabin is as loud as ever. The cancelling happens only in the small pocket of air between the speaker and your eardrum. Lift a cup and the engine is back.
