The switch

Insulin decides: store or release

After a meal insulin rises and the body stores. Between meals it falls and the body draws on its stores.

After a meal

13:45 · insulin high
  • Muscles pull sugar in
  • Liver and muscles refill glycogen
  • Fat stays locked away

Between meals

12:30 · insulin low
  • Liver releases sugar to keep blood steady
  • Liver glycogen slowly drains
  • Fat opens up and becomes the main fuel

Under the hood

How the twin works

A published glucose–insulin model sits at the core. Teaching layers for glycogen, fat, exercise, sleep and stress sit around it. Every layer is a simplification.

Core · Dalla Man, Rizza & Cobelli 2007

Glucose, insulin and the pancreas

The UVA/Padova meal model, average healthy subject. Its insulin action on muscle, X, is the "remote insulin" of Bergman's minimal model.

glucose, mg/kg G = Gp / VG dGp/dt = EGP + Ra − Uii − E − k1·Gp + k2·Gt dGt/dt = −Uid + k1·Gp − k2·Gt uptake (Bergman's X) Uid = (Vm0 + Vmx·X) · Gt / (Km0 + Gt) dX/dt = −p2U·X + p2U·(I − Ib) liver output (never below 0) EGP = kp1 − kp2·Gp − kp3·Id − kp4·Ipo insulin, pmol/kg I = Ip / VI dIl/dt = −(m1 + m3)·Il + m2·Ip + S dIp/dt = −(m2 + m4)·Ip + m1·Il pancreas S = γ·Ipo, dIpo/dt = −γ·Ipo + Y + K·max(dG/dt, 0) + Sb dY/dt = −α·(Y − β·(G − Gb))
Gut · Dalla Man, Camilleri & Cobelli 2006

Stomach, gut and the speed of a meal

Food is ground, emptied and absorbed. Emptying is fast, then slow, then fast again. "Fast", "medium" and "slow" foods scale these rates. Liquids skip grinding.

dQsto1/dt = −kgri·Qsto1 + meal dQsto2/dt = kgri·Qsto1 − kempt·Qsto2 dQgut/dt = kempt·Qsto2 − kabs·Qgut Ra = f·kabs·Qgut / BW kempt = kmin + (kmax − kmin)/2 · [tanh(a·(Qsto − b·D)) − tanh(c·(Qsto − d·D)) + 2] teaching layer: absorption ceilings glucose ≤ 1.2 g/min (SGLT1) fructose ≤ 0.6 g/min (GLUT5) fructose → liver → 50% glucose · 30% glycogen · 20% burned
Teaching layer

Glycogen and fat

Stores are bookkeeping on top of the core fluxes.

muscle glycogen (legs and rest of body) store = 0.55 · (Uid − Uid_fasting) · room left in tank burn = exercise draw (see Exercise) liver glycogen dL/dt = first-pass sugar + gluconeogenesis − EGP EGP ≤ gluconeogenesis + 9·L/(L+4) ← empties → only GNG GNG ≈ 0.85 mg/kg/min, higher when fasting, exercising, stressed or low on sugar fat burned (indirect calorimetry logic) fat = (energy − 4·carbs − 4·protein) / 9
Teaching layer · after Roy & Parker 2007, Breton 2008

Exercise and the wall

Pace sets intensity and cost. Carbs take a bigger share as intensity rises. When a fuel hits its ceiling, pace drops.

intensity = VO2(pace) / VDOT Daniels & Gilbert cost ≈ 0.9 kcal per kg per km carb share = 0.10 + 0.90 / (1 + e^−(intensity − crossover)/0.14) blood-sugar share rises as legs empty and when fed leg glycogen ceiling = 4.0 · M² / (M² + (0.27·Mcap)²) g/min fat ceiling = 0.30–0.62 g/min by fitness, less when insulin is up fastest pace = 4·(glycogen + blood sugar) + 9·fat ceilings blood sugar < 75 mg/dL → the brain cuts effort (the bonk) effort reserve ≈ 1.5% × predicted time critical-speed idea drains above the intensity held for the predicted race empty → pace capped at 96% of predicted race pace after exercise: insulin sensitivity up, τ ≈ 10 h
Teaching layer

Sleep, stress and the body clock

sleep (Donga 2010; Spiegel 1999) insulin sensitivity × (1 − 0.065 per hour under 7.5 h) stress (Shamoon 1981; cortisol effects) adrenaline-like E, τ 8 min: liver output ↑, insulin release ↓ cortisol-like C, τ 25–70 min, its effect τ 200 min: insulin sensitivity × (1 − 0.55·effect) body clock (Saad 2012) insulin sensitivity × (1 + 0.15·cos(2π(h − 10)/24)) asleep: muscle sensitivity × 0.80
What it leaves out

Limits worth knowing

  • One "average" body. Real people vary a lot, often by 2× or more.
  • Healthy metabolism only. Not built for diabetes, pregnancy or medication.
  • Glucagon, free fatty acids and lactate are folded into simple rules.
  • Fat and protein in a meal only change its speed, not the insulin response.
  • Heat, hydration, hills and muscle damage are not in race mode.
  • Fatigue is mostly about fuel here. In real races, it rarely is only fuel.
