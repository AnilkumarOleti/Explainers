Announced after the fact

Who really decided?

Brain science suggests the voice in your head that says “I chose” works like a press secretary. It announces decisions made in a back room, a moment later, and believes it made them.

The back room is the brain doing the actual work, out of sight.

The envelope is the decision, already made.

You are the part that reads it out and feels like its author.

Exhibit A · 1983 · Benjamin Libet

The brain moves first

Volunteers flicked a finger whenever they felt like it, and noted on a fast clock the moment they felt the urge. Electrodes on the scalp watched the brain.

The press secretary gets the envelope after the brain has started moving.
Exhibit B · 2008 · Haynes lab, Berlin

A scanner guessed your choice before you did

People pressed left or right, whenever they liked. Brain activity predicted which button, long before they felt they had picked. Catch: the guess was right about 60% of the time. A coin gets 50%.

Exhibit C · 2009 · Desmurget, Lyon

Poke the brain, and a “want” appears

During brain surgery, a mild current in one spot made patients feel a desire to move. Turn it up, and they believed they had moved when they hadn’t. A different spot moved their hand, and they denied it. The feeling of willing and the act can be pulled apart with a wire.

Exhibit D · 1970s onward · Gazzaniga, split-brain patients

The narrator makes up reasons

In these patients the two brain halves can’t talk to each other. The talking half never saw the snow, yet it explained the shovel instantly. In another study, people happily defended a photo they had not picked; most never noticed the swap.

The big argument · Robert Sapolsky, “Determined” (2023)

Every choice has a cause. So does that cause.

Culture

Neurons, right now
Your “choice”
Neurons fire because of chemistry. Nobody picked their genes or childhood. So where would a free, uncaused choice slip in?
The other side

Why many scientists aren’t convinced

The ramp may be noise

A 2012 study argued Libet’s rising brain signal is random background drift that tips over a line, not a hidden decision.

You can still veto

People can cancel a movement until about 0.2 seconds before it happens. Libet himself called this “free won’t.”

60% isn’t mind reading

And pressing a random button isn’t choosing a career or a partner, where you weigh reasons for days.

“Free” may mean something else

Many philosophers (compatibilists, like Daniel Dennett) say free will means acting on your own reasons, without a gun to your head. Causes don’t rule that out.

Where that leaves us

The scorecard

Well supported
  • The brain gets to work before you feel you’ve decided.
  • The feeling of “I willed it” can be created or removed.
  • Your mind invents reasons after the fact, and believes them.
Still open
  • Whether conscious thinking can steer the back room over longer decisions.
  • Whether any of this rules out the everyday meaning of “free.”

The press secretary is real. What’s argued is whether it ever sends notes back.
