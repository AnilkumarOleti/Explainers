The rule

Of everything that glitters, how much is gold?

9 9+89 = 9.2%

That fraction is Bayes’ rule. Written in symbols:

P(gold | glitter)= P(glitter | gold) × P(gold)P(glitter)
PriorHow much of the river is gold, before you look.1.0%
LikelihoodHow often gold glitters.90%
EvidenceHow much of the river glitters at all.9.8%
PosteriorHow much of the glitter is gold. Your answer.9.2%

The same sum as odds, which is quicker in your head:

1 : 99gold to gravel, before
× 10times likelier to glitter if gold
≈ 9 : 89gold to fool’s gold, after

Numbers are made up to show the pattern. A weak clue about a common thing beats a strong clue about a rare one.

Before you trust a clue, ask how rare the thing is.

Thomas Bayes worked this out in the 1700s. His essay was published in 1763, two years after his death.
