A reflection in three movements

The Same Day

Your day today, set beside the documented day of a registered prisoner at Auschwitz, 1940–1945.

This is a way to notice what you had today, and to remember the people from whom all of it was taken. Every statement here about the camp comes from the Auschwitz-Birkenau Memorial, the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum or Yad Vashem, and carries a small numbered marker you can tap to see its source.

Before you begin

Most people deported to Auschwitz never lived a day there. Of about 1.3 million people deported, a little over 400,000 were registered as prisoners. Most Jewish deportees were murdered in the gas chambers on arrival. Around 1.1 million people were killed at Auschwitz in total.

The day described here is that of a registered prisoner, which was itself the exception.

This takes about 10 minutes. There is nothing to win.

    Something else

    Calories, drawn to scale

      kcal per day

      • On paper the day’s food was half a litre of ersatz “coffee” or herbal “tea” in the morning, about a litre of soup at noon (potatoes, swede, a little groats), and in the evening about 300–350 g of bread with a little margarine, sausage, marmalade or cheese.
      • In theory a loaf of about 1 kg was shared among four prisoners. The functionaries handing it out often cut slices for themselves first.
      • Details varied. No official menu survives for the main camp or Birkenau; a menu from one sub-camp lists meat soup four times a week.
      • Official norms put the day’s food at about 1,700 kcal for light work and 2,150 for heavy labour. In practice, prisoners received around 1,300 kcal for lighter work and 1,700 for hard labour.
      • The thicker part of the soup settled at the bottom of the pot and usually went to functionaries, who sometimes sold or traded it. SS guards also stole from the rations.
      • The evening bread was meant to last until morning. Many ate it at once, partly for fear another starving prisoner would take it in the night.
      • A modern estimate for a young man of 70 kg living a heavily active life is about 3,900 kcal a day. After several weeks on camp rations, most prisoners began to waste away, a state that often ended in death or in selection for the gas chambers.

      You chose what you ate today. That choice is the difference.

      What will you do with today?

      Nothing here was a test, and nothing was added up. The comforts on this page are ordinary on purpose. They were taken from real people, each with a name, a family, work and plans, and most of those deported to Auschwitz were not given even one of these days.

      Viktor Frankl, a psychiatrist who survived Auschwitz and other camps, wrote that a person can be stripped of almost everything except one freedom: to choose the attitude they bring to what happens to them.

      He did not claim that attitude decided who survived. It is a reminder of what is still yours today, along with everything you ticked above.
