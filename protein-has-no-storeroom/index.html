Protein and muscle building

Can you eat a week's protein in one day?

No. Protein has no storeroom.

Your body banks fat for weeks and carbohydrate for about a day. It banks no protein at all. Whatever a day delivers beyond what the body can put to work is burned as fuel, and on a day with none it takes amino acids back out of its own tissue. For building muscle, eat your protein every day. Inside a single day you have far more freedom over when.

The same weekly total, split seven ways

Picture muscle as a brick wall and protein as the bricks. The site has no storeroom, so bricks that are not laid the day they arrive go into the furnace. Move the slider to change how many days a week the truck comes.

Why there is no storeroom

The body stores energy in two places. Fat tissue holds weeks of it. Glycogen in the liver and muscle holds about a day. Protein has nothing like either. The only loose supply is the free amino acids dissolved in blood and cells, roughly 100 g in a 70 kg adult, which is less than one day's target. Every other gram of protein in the body is busy being something: muscle fibre, enzyme, skin, antibody.

What happens to a brick

The wall is never finished. Each day an adult breaks down and rebuilds roughly 300 g of body protein, several times what they eat. Most of those bricks are reused. Some are lost on every cycle, and food replaces them.

Amino acids that arrive with no building work waiting have one exit. The nitrogen is stripped off and leaves as urea. What remains is burned for energy or turned into glucose or fat.

New muscle takes very little. Muscle is about one-fifth protein and mostly water, so a beginner adding a kilogram of muscle in a month is adding about 7 g of protein a day to the wall. The rest of a day's intake pays for upkeep. There is no job big enough to absorb a week's delivery at once.

On a day with no delivery the repair work still runs. The body then draws on the biggest supply it has, which is muscle.

Training opens a window about a day and a half long

A hard session raises the rate of muscle building for a long time afterwards. In one classic measurement the rate was 50% above rest at 4 hours, 109% above at 24 hours, and close to normal again by 36 hours. Train three times a week and nearly every day falls inside a window. Each window needs bricks on hand while it is open, which includes rest days.

Inside a day, you have room

The old rule that the body can only use 20 to 30 g of protein per meal has not held up. A large meal digests slowly, so it behaves like a slow delivery that lasts most of a day. That is the freedom you get. It covers hours, and it stops well short of a week.

  • Trommelen et al., 2023100 g vs 25 g of milk protein after one session

    The 100 g meal produced a larger response and kept it going for more than 12 hours, with a negligible rise in amino acids burned. This trial used about one day's protein. It shows a day's worth can arrive in one sitting. It does not show seven days' worth can.

  • Time-restricted eating trialsAll food in an 8-hour window, with lifting

    When protein is matched at about 1.4 to 1.6 g per kg, lean mass holds up as well as it does with a normal eating pattern.

  • Yasuda et al., 202012 weeks of lifting at 1.3 g per kg a day

    Young men who spread protein evenly over three meals gained 2.5 kg of lean mass. Those who ate little of it at breakfast gained 1.8 kg. The difference fell just short of statistical significance (p = 0.06).

  • Morton et al., 201849 trials, 1,863 people

    Gains in lean mass level off at about 1.6 g per kg a day, with a confidence range of 1.0 to 2.2.

Read together, the daily total does most of the work. An even split across meals may add a little, mostly when the total is on the low side.

What to do, in order of how much it matters

  1. Train hard and keep adding load.

    Protein builds nothing without the signal from training.

  2. Hit a daily total of about 1.6 g per kg of body weight.

    Anywhere from 1.2 to 2.2 g per kg is a sensible working range for someone who lifts.

    At 70 kg that is about 112 g a day.

  3. Do it every day, rest days included.

    The window from yesterday's session is still open, and upkeep never stops.

  4. If it suits you, split the total over three or four meals.

    About 0.4 g per kg at each of four meals reaches the daily figure. One or two large meals also work if that is how you eat.

    At 70 kg that is about 28 g a meal.

  5. Leave a missed day behind.

    A shortfall cannot be banked or repaid in bulk. Go back to your usual amount the next day. One low day costs very little. Six a week costs a lot.

How sure is this

Nobody has run a trial of one protein day a week, and nobody is likely to. For a 70 kg person it means about 11 g per kg in a single day, well over twice the highest estimates of what the liver can process in a day. Those estimates are themselves debated, and nausea would settle the matter before the chemistry did.

So the answer here is reasoned from how protein metabolism works, plus trials on meal patterns inside a day. The direction is not in doubt. The exact gram figures in the brick model are illustrative.

The 1.6 g per kg figure is for healthy adults who lift. Anyone with kidney disease should set their protein intake with their doctor.
