The score

Every position becomes one number

Plus means White is better. 1 point ≈ one extra pawn.

Old engines

Hand-written rules. Knight ≈ 3 pawns, rook ≈ 5, queen ≈ 9. Safe king, active pieces.

Modern engines

A neural network that learned from billions of positions. Intuition, checked by brute calculation.

Why engines win

Six unfair advantages

Raw speed

Tens of millions of positions a second on a home PC. In 1997, Deep Blue checked 200 million a second to beat Kasparov.

No hope chess

Always assumes the opponent finds the best reply. Traps don’t work on it.

No nerves

No fatigue, no fear, no ego. Move 80 gets the same care as move 1.

Pure objectivity

No favourite plans. It drops an idea the moment the number says so.

Solved endings

Every position with 7 or fewer pieces is already solved. It just looks up the answer.

Endless practice

Learns by playing itself, millions of games. In 2017, AlphaZero taught itself chess this way in hours.

The result

Strength, on one ruler

Chess ratings. Every 400 points ≈ the stronger side scores about 90%.

Engine ratings come from engine-vs-engine games, so the gap is an estimate. Top humans no longer play top engines; the result isn’t in doubt.

In one line

Humans ask “what’s the plan?”
Engines ask “what’s the number?”

And more computer power means more light on the tree. That’s the whole edge.
