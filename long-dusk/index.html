Part one · The body

The light leaves in stages

Your body is a building with many tenants. When the heart stops, they do not all leave at once. Drag the time and watch the floors go dark.

Nautical twilight · sun at −8.3°

6 minutes since the heart stopped

Without help, brain cells start to die. After 2 to 5 minutes with no pulse, doctors may declare death.

Typical figures for an adult at normal body temperature. Cold slows every stage. The twilight names are the picture, the timings are the biology.

So when is someone dead?

A dusk has no exact end, so medicine draws two lines across it.

The heart line

No pulse, no breath, and neither will return. Doctors wait 2 to 5 minutes to be sure.

The brain line

The whole brain has stopped for good, even while a ventilator keeps the heart beating. First defined in 1968. Indian law has recognised it since 1994.

Why a cell goes dark

Every floor fails the same way. Only the speed differs.

  1. Oxygen stopsNo blood flow, no delivery.
  2. Fuel runs outThe cell's battery, ATP, is spent in minutes.
  3. Pumps failSalt and water flood in. The cell swells.
  4. It digests itselfIts own enzymes break the walls.

Why we die at all

Evolution only guards you while you can still pass on genes.

  1. A gene that kills you young gets weeded out fast.
  2. A gene that harms you at 70 has already been passed on.
  3. So bodies are built to last long enough. Repairs get skipped, damage piles up.

Part two · The stories

Every culture has a story for the night

Thousands of traditions, six basic answers. Pick one.

    Grief gets a calendar

    Whatever the story, a funeral does three jobs: care for the body, send the person on, and stitch the living back together. Almost everywhere, that work is scheduled.

    Days after a death, on a scale that compresses as it goes (each step is about ten times longer). Customs vary by community.

    We are the animal that can read the clock

    Every creature runs from danger. Only one knows the ending in advance.

    A dread no other animal carries

    The idea is Ernest Becker's, from The Denial of Death (1973).

    So we build two shelters

    Terror management theory says most of culture doubles as a roof over this dread.

    A story that outlasts me

    Faith, nation, family, art, science. Something that was here before me and will go on.

    A part in it

    Self-esteem: the feeling that I count for something inside that story.

    The famous test, 1989. Twenty-two judges set bail for the same case. Half were first asked to write about their own death.

    Which part scares you?

    The fear of death is really several fears. Each has a different answer.

    What seems to help

    • Look at itMonks, Stoics and hospice workers agree: death scares less the more often you face it.
    • Close the loopsSay the thing. Write the will. Fear feeds on what is left undone.
    • Belong to something longerChildren, students, a craft, a cause. Anything that carries on.
    • Trust the ending moreYour brain files death under "other people", so your picture of it is poor. With good care, most endings are calmer than imagined.
    Dusk is what makes it a day.

    The same mind that can see the ending is the one that gets to decide what the day is for.
