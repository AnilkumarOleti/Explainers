Sheet A-01Subject Residential flatContext India · Hyderabad

Reading a Property

You aren't buying a flat. You're buying six things stacked on top of each other, and a site visit only shows you the top two.

What buyers usually do

Inspect the top

The kitchen, the view, the clubhouse, the sample flat. These are the easiest to see and the easiest to change later.

What decides the outcome

Inspect the bottom

Location and title can't be renovated. A bad unit costs you money to fix. A bad title or a bad location costs you the property's value.

How to use this sheet

Check the layers in the order below. Each one is a filter. The early ones are cheap to check and eliminate the most options, so you don't fall in love with a flat before finding out its land title is disputed. Paper is a kill switch: one failure there ends the conversation no matter how good the rest is.

01 Can't be changed, ever

Location

A building depreciates. The land under it is what appreciates, and land value is almost entirely location.

Commute at peak hour

Drive the route at 9 am and 7 pm on a weekday, not on a Sunday with the sales team. A 20-minute Sunday drive is often 55 minutes on a Tuesday.

What's the travel time to work, school and a hospital at rush hour?

Water

Where does water come from: municipal supply, borewells, or tankers? Tanker dependence is a monthly cost and a summer risk that no brochure mentions.

What did the society spend on tankers last summer?

Flooding and lakes

Is the plot low-lying or near a lake, nala or stormwater drain? Visit after heavy rain if you can. Check whether the land falls in a lake's full tank level (FTL) or buffer zone.

Is any part of the survey number inside an FTL or buffer boundary?

What gets built next

The empty land around you will not stay empty. Look at the master plan for road widening, and count the upcoming projects nearby. They compete with you on resale and rent.

What does the master plan zone the surrounding plots as?

Hyderabad note

Since 2024, HYDRAA has demolished structures built on lake FTL and buffer land, some of which had approvals on paper. Lake proximity is now a title question as much as a flooding one.

02 Kill switch

Paper

You don't own what you can see. You own what the documents say, and a property lawyer should read them before you pay any significant amount.

RERA registration

Every new project above a size threshold must be registered with the state RERA (TG-RERA in Telangana). The registration page lists approvals, timelines, complaints and quarterly progress.

No number, walk
Title chain

The sequence of ownership of the land for the last 30 years, ending in the person or builder selling to you. Gaps, disputes or unregistered transfers are the classic failure.

Lawyer reads
Encumbrance certificate

Shows loans, mortgages and legal charges registered against the property. You want it clean, or with loans that the seller will close before registration.

Must have
Approved plan

Sanctioned building plan from the local authority (GHMC or HMDA in Hyderabad). Compare it with what's actually built: extra floors or converted parking are unauthorised.

Match to site
Land use

The land must be approved for residential use. Agricultural land needs a formal conversion before a building on it is legal.

Must have
Occupancy certificate

Issued when a building is complete and matches its approvals. Without it, water and power connections, loans and resale all get harder.

Ready flats
Prior sale deeds

For a resale flat: every previous sale deed, the society's no-objection letter, paid property tax receipts, and proof that the seller's home loan is closed.

Resale
A useful shortcut, not a substitute

Ask which banks have approved the project for home loans. Banks do their own legal check first, so several large lenders on the list is a good sign. It still doesn't replace your own lawyer.

03 For under-construction flats, the product is a promise

Builder

When you buy off-plan, you're lending money to a company for three or four years. Judge it the way a lender would: by what it has already delivered.

Visit an old project

Go to a building this builder finished 5 to 8 years ago. Look at the lift lobbies, seepage stains, and the state of the common areas. Talk to residents at the gate.

Was it handed over on time, and did it match the brochure?

Delivery record

Compare promised and actual possession dates across their past projects. The RERA site shows the extensions each project has asked for.

How many past projects were delayed by more than a year?

Complaints

Search RERA complaints and consumer court cases under the builder's name and the project name. A few complaints are normal for a large builder. A pattern is not.

What are the complaints about: delays, quality, or refunds?

Construction pace

How far along is the site versus the payment you're being asked for? Construction-linked payment plans should track real progress.

Which slab is done, and what percentage have buyers already paid?

04 Shared with everyone for decades

Building

The common parts decide your daily life and your monthly maintenance bill for as long as you live there.

Density and UDS

Divide the plot area by the number of flats. Fewer flats per acre means more open space, less queueing, and a bigger undivided share of land (UDS) registered in your name. UDS is the part that appreciates.

What UDS is registered for this flat, in square yards?

Lifts and parking

Count flats per lift. Beyond about 4 flats per lift per floor in a tall tower, morning waits get long. Check whether your car park is covered, allotted and in writing.

How many lifts serve this core, and how many are service lifts?

Maintenance cost

Large amenity lists mean large maintenance bills. Ask for the per-sq-ft rate and the corpus fund amount now, before they're decided for you.

What will maintenance be after the first free year?

Power and water backup

Check DG backup coverage (full or lifts-only), the sewage treatment plant, and rainwater harvesting. These are expensive to add later.

Is power backup 100% or only for common areas?

05 The part everyone looks at

Unit

Look at it hard, but know which flaws are fixable. Tiles can be changed. Orientation, light and layout cannot.

Carpet area
The usable floor inside your walls. Under RERA, builders must state the sale in carpet area. This is the number to compare flats on.
Built-up area
Carpet plus the thickness of walls and your balconies.
Super built-up area
Built-up plus your share of corridors, lifts, staircases and the clubhouse. Most price quotes are still per sq ft of this. The gap to carpet is the loading, often 25–35%.

Light and heat

West-facing rooms take the afternoon sun, which matters in a Hyderabad May. Stand in the flat at 3 pm if you can. Check which rooms get cross-ventilation.

Which direction do the bedrooms and balcony face?

Usable shape

Two flats with the same carpet area can feel very different. Angled walls, long corridors inside the flat, and columns in rooms waste space you're paying for.

Can a queen bed and wardrobe fit in the second bedroom?

What overlooks you

Look out of every window. A clear view today may face the next tower's kitchen balconies in three years. Note what's on the adjacent plot.

What's approved to be built on the plot next door?

Signs of trouble

For a ready or resale flat: damp patches, stains under windows, cracks near beams, water pressure on the top floor, and the smell of the drains.

Can I open every tap and flush every toilet?

06 Last, because it only means something after 01–05

Price

The quoted rate is per square foot of space you can't use, before the extras and before tax. The only honest number is all-in cost per square foot of carpet.

Compare registered prices

Listing prices are asks. Registered sale values for the same building or street are what people actually paid. The state registration department and property portals publish these.

What did the last three flats in this project register at?

Check the rent

Residential yields in Indian cities usually sit around 2–4% a year. If the rent a flat could fetch implies a yield far below that, you're paying for expected appreciation, not for the home.

What do similar flats here rent for today, not after completion?

Stamp duty basis

Stamp duty is charged on the agreement value or the government guidance value for the area, whichever is higher. Rates change; confirm the current figure with your lawyer or the sub-registrar before budgeting.

× Architects mark errors in red. So should you.

Red lines

No RERA number on the ad

New projects must display it. Missing means unregistered, which means no regulator to complain to.

More than 10% before the agreement

RERA bars builders from taking over 10% of the price before a registered agreement for sale. A demand for more is a demand to break the law.

A cash component

Any part of the price paid off the books is unprotected, can't be recovered in a dispute, and lowers your registered value for future resale.

"OC is coming"

A finished building without an occupancy certificate may have deviated from its plan. Don't pay full price for a promise.

Price quoted only on super built-up

If the builder won't tell you the carpet area in writing, assume the loading is on the high side.

Pressure to decide today

"Last unit at this price" is a sales script. A good property survives a week of document checks.
