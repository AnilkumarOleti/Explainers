    The case fans make

    Why people call it the best ever made

    “Best ever” is a matter of taste, but the argument for Breaking Bad rests on six things it did better than almost anything before it.

    Arc

    It knew where it was going

    Most long shows drift. This one was built as a single five-season transformation, and it ended on its own terms in 2013, when it was more popular than ever. No limp final years, no cancellation cliffhanger.

    Consequence

    Everything comes back

    Walt lets Jane die in season 2. Her grieving father, an air traffic controller, makes a mistake, and two planes collide over Albuquerque. Actions in this show ripple outward, sometimes seasons later.

    Complicity

    It made you root for him, then asked why

    You cheer Walt standing up to bullies in season 1. By season 5 you’re watching a man poison a child. The show slowly makes the audience part of the moral experiment.

    Craft

    It told stories with pictures

    Cold opens that flash forward, colour-coded costumes, cameras mounted inside trunks and at the bottom of holes, time-lapse desert skies. You could follow a lot of it on mute.

    Performance

    A cast at full strength

    Cranston won four lead-actor Emmys, Aaron Paul three, Anna Gunn two. Giancarlo Esposito turned a fried-chicken franchise owner into one of TV’s scariest villains while barely raising his voice.

    Peak

    It got better as it went

    Most shows peak early. Breaking Bad’s final eight episodes are its best reviewed, and “Ozymandias,” the third-to-last episode, is often called the finest hour of television ever made.

    The case against

    Not everyone puts it first

    On major critics’ lists it usually lands in the top three, but the other two have strong defenders.

    The Sopranos

    The original antihero

    Tony Soprano came first. Walt is his direct descendant, and many critics rank the show that invented the form above the one that perfected its plot.

    The Wire

    A whole city, not one man

    Breaking Bad is about one person’s decay. The Wire studies an entire city’s institutions, and its fans argue that is the harder and bigger achievement.

    Better Call Saul

    The spinoff that rivals it

    A real faction thinks the prequel is the better-made show: slower, subtler and more patient. It never won an Emmy in 53 nominations, which fans treat as a scandal.

    Its own flaws

    Slow start, ugly fandom

    Season 1 is short and uneven. Part of the audience cheered Heisenberg rather than seeing a warning, and the hatred aimed at Skyler got so bad that Anna Gunn wrote a New York Times op-ed about it in 2013.

    The fairest verdict: few shows have ever planned a story this tightly and then stuck the landing. If “best” means the most complete, controlled piece of storytelling, Breaking Bad has one of the strongest claims there is.
