Where it started · Haiti, 1700s

Born on a sugar plantation

Enslaved Africans in Haiti feared a sorcerer could trap your soul and work your body forever, even after death.

The horror wasn't being eaten. It was being owned.

The word likely comes from a Central African word for a spirit.

The cockroach on a leash

A jewel wasp stings a cockroach in the brain. The roach can still walk, but loses the will to run.

The wasp leads it home by the antenna, like a dog on a leash, to feed its baby.

Zombie cells, inside you

Old, damaged cells stop doing their job, but refuse to die. They leak alarm signals that inflame the healthy cells around them.

We pile up more of them as we age. Scientists are testing drugs that clear them out.

Zombie companies

A company that can't earn enough to even pay the interest on its loans, three years in a row.

New loans keep it on a drip. It holds on to workers and money that healthier firms could use.

Someone else driving

Zombie computers

Hidden malware takes over a laptop, router or home camera. It still seems normal to you, but a stranger can wake thousands at once.

In 2016, an army of hijacked cameras and routers called Mirai knocked big sites like Twitter and Netflix offline for hours.

Programmers also have "zombie processes": finished programs that nobody cleared away.
