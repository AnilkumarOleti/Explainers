Goodhart's law, in pictures

Every KPI eventually lies.

You wanted a warm room. You hung a thermometer. Then you rewarded people when it read high.

What a KPI is

A small window onto a big thing.

You can't see every customer. So you watch one number that usually moves with them.

The window is useful. It is not the room.

"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."

Goodhart's law, in anthropologist Marilyn Strathern's wording
How it happens

Four ways a number drifts from the truth.

The match

Move the reading, not the thing.

"Tickets closed in 5 min" goes up. Because agents close tickets without solving them.

The spotlight

Only the lit part gets better.

Test scores rise. Reading doesn't. Teachers taught the test.

The finger

The number becomes the goal. People forget what it pointed at.

"Grow time spent." Nobody asks if people liked that time.

The old map

The world moved. The number didn't.

"Calls answered fast": perfect. Customers left the phone for chat long ago.

This is old news

People have always gamed the count.

Hanoi, 1902

Paid per rat tail. People cut the tails and let the rats go to breed.

Delhi, as the story goes

A bounty on cobras. People bred cobras. More snakes than before.

Wells Fargo, 2016

Sales targets per customer. Staff opened millions of accounts nobody asked for.

An old Soviet joke

Count nails: millions of tiny ones. Weigh nails: one giant one.

What to do

Keep your numbers honest.

You can't stop the drift. You can slow it and spot it.

Pair it

Give every number a partner that pulls the other way.

Speed + reopen rate. Signups + 30-day retention.

Go look

Read ten real cases behind the number, every month.

If the stories and the number disagree, trust the stories.

Watch, don't pay

Keep some numbers for learning only. No bonus attached.

A gauge nobody profits from stays honest longer.

Swap it

Change or retire a number once people learn to play it.

If it only ever goes up, ask why.

The one thing to remember

The number is a hint.
Go feel the room.

A KPI is great at noticing change and bad at being a goal. The harder you lean on it, the faster it bends.
